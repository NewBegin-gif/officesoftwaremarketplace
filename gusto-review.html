What buyers report about Gusto

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Gusto ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • 4.6 on G2 across more than 9,000 reviews and ranked first on G2's Highest Satisfaction Products list for 2026.
  • • Running payroll, onboarding someone and giving employees self-service are all genuinely easy — that is what reviewers name.
What they complain about
  • • The Simple plan base fee went from $40 to $49 a month in early 2026, plus $6 per employee. That increase narrowed the gap to cheaper rivals — OnPay now matches it on base price.
  • • Support on the lower tiers is inconsistent, and reviewers say it is exactly when something has gone wrong that it fails.
  • • The mobile app trails the desktop and reporting flexibility is limited.

Where the ratings disagree. 4.6 on both G2 and Capterra against roughly 2.4-2.5 on Trustpilot across 2,364 reviews. The Trustpilot cluster is billing disputes and support delays — the experience when payroll goes wrong, which is not what the average G2 reviewer is rating.

When the paid plan is worth it

Every tier is a base fee plus a per-employee charge, and which half dominates flips completely with headcount. Our dated reading: Simple about $49 a month plus $6 an employee, Plus $80 plus $12, Premium $180 plus $22, and a contractor-only plan from about $35 plus $6. Run those at real team sizes and the base fee turns out to be a small-team tax. On Simple, five employees costs about $79 — $15.80 a head, of which 62% is the base fee you would pay for one employee.

Twenty employees costs $169, or $8.45 a head with the base down to 29%. Fifty costs $349: $6.98 a head, base 14%. The effective per-employee price therefore more than halves between five and fifty people without you changing anything. The same shape holds on every tier — Premium runs $58 a head at five employees and $25.60 at fifty. The consequence for choosing a tier: the jump from Simple to Plus costs $31 of base plus $6 an employee, so at twenty people it is $151 more a month, and at fifty it is $331.

Tier decisions get expensive as you grow in a way the headline does not show, which argues for deciding what you actually need from Plus and Premium while the team is small and the gap is cheap. One limit on all of the above, stated because it matters: Gusto blocks plain fetches of its pricing page and neither our July nor our August 2026 capture retrieved an amount. These are our dated figures rather than independently confirmed ones — check them against your own quote.

And the structural caveat no tier changes: Gusto is US-only, so an international team is outside what this product does at any price.

How to actually use Gusto

Confirm the current prices yourself before you budget anything. Gusto blocks plain fetches of its pricing page and neither of our 2026 captures retrieved an amount, so our figures are dated readings rather than confirmed rates. Then model at your real headcount, because the base-plus-per-employee structure changes shape as you grow. On our reading, twenty employees costs about $169 a month on Simple ($49 base plus $6 a head), $320 on Plus and $620 on Premium — and in every case roughly three quarters of the bill is the per-head component, not the base.

Comparing base fees between payroll providers is therefore close to meaningless; compare the total at your headcount. Check the geography before anything else, though. Gusto is US-focused throughout. If you have anyone on payroll outside the United States, or you run multiple entities, this is the wrong tool regardless of price — and if you only have contractors, ask about the contractor-only plan, which starts far lower.

Frequently asked questions

What does Gusto cost?

Our dated reading puts Simple at about $49 a month plus $6 per employee, Plus at $80 plus $12, and Premium at $180 plus $22, with a contractor-only plan from about $35 plus $6 per contractor. We could not re-confirm those from the page on 17 August 2026 because it blocks plain fetches, so treat them as indicative.

How does the price scale with headcount?

Faster than the base fee suggests. On Simple, twenty employees adds $120 a month to a $49 base; on Premium the same headcount adds $440 to $180. The per-employee component is what you are really buying, so compare providers on that number rather than on the monthly base.

Is it usable outside the US?

Not really — Gusto is built around US payroll, tax filing and benefits. If you employ people in other countries, this is the wrong shape of product and the comparison is with an employer-of-record service instead.

What should I weigh before buying Financial Operations software?

The bar here is higher than features. Check who actually holds the money and under what licence, whether client funds are segregated, what audit trail and permission controls you get, and how support behaves when something goes wrong mid-transaction. Anything touching payments should be able to answer those in writing; if it cannot, that is your answer.
