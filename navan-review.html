Who it is for

Best for companies that want self-serve travel booking with built-in policy control — genuinely free — and optionally unified expense management on top.

Skip it if your travel volume is a few trips a year (any booking site does), or you need expense-only tooling without the travel layer.

What to weigh it against

The comparison that matters is TravelPerk or Ramp (for expense) — TravelPerk is the direct travel rival; Ramp attacks the expense side — Navan's edge is the free, unified travel core.

Questions buyers actually ask

Is Navan really free?

The travel product is, to you: it is funded by commissions from travel suppliers rather than by a subscription, and that covers booking, policy enforcement and basic expense capture. The Expense product is free for the first 15 users and charged per user after that. Navan publishes no price list, so the per-user figure comes from a quote.

What is the catch with commission-funded travel software?

Not a hidden fee — an incentive. The platform earns when you book through it, so the thing to test is whether your own negotiated hotel and airline rates are loaded and surfaced, and whether the policy engine steers travellers toward inventory that pays Navan. Ask for that in the demo with your own routes.

When does it stop being free?

At the sixteenth user of the Expense product, which is where per-user billing starts. If you only need travel booking and policy, the free scope genuinely covers it; if expense management is the reason you are looking, price the seats from the start.
