Deputy Review (2026)$30 Monthly Floor Under Six Users

By Daan Haket · Tested hands-on · written with an AI assistant · who does what

The minimum monthly spend is the number to check before anything else: at $5.50 per user on monthly billing, a team of fewer than about six people still pays the $30 floor, so the headline per-user price is misleading at small scale.

Best for

shift-based businesses — hospitality, retail, healthcare, any hourly workforce — that need rostering, clocking and labour-law compliance in one system and have enough staff to clear the minimum spend.

Skip it if

you run a small salaried desk team: the $30 monthly minimum means you pay for roughly six users whether you have them or not, and salaried staff rarely need shift-level clocking at all.

When the paid plan is worth it

Do the add-on arithmetic before you pick Core, because on the published rates the tier above it is cheaper. Annual per user: Lite $5, Core $6.50, Pro $9. Messaging+ costs $1.95 a user and Analytics+ $1.50, and both are included in Pro. So Core with those two is $9.95 a user against $9 for Pro -- ninety-five cents a head more for less. If you want either of those add-ons, price Pro first.

The catch is that Pro is not self-serve: Lite and Core start with a free trial while Pro says Contact us, so the cheaper option requires a conversation. Worth having that conversation with the sum above in hand. Two things that hit small teams harder than the per-user rate suggests. Monthly billing carries a minimum spend of USD $30 (AUD $30, GBP 20) per invoice on Lite, Core and Pro, so a team of three on monthly Lite pays $30 rather than $16.50 -- an effective $10 a head, double the list price.

Annual billing has no such floor and is cheaper per user anyway ($5 against $5.50 on Lite). And Deputy Payroll through Paycor is $8 a user PLUS a $49 monthly base fee, available only on annual Core and Pro in the US -- that base fee alone is more than ten Lite seats.

How to actually use Deputy

Setup follows Deputy's own hierarchy, and getting it wrong is what forces a rebuild later: the Organisation at the top, then Locations, then Areas. A Location needs a real street address rather than a label, because Deputy uses it to show where a shift was started and ended; Areas are the parts of a location staff are scheduled into, so they determine what a roster can look like. Team members go in with a name and an access level, and the email address is documented as optional.

But the same documentation is explicit that without one they cannot create an account with your organisation, which means no clock-in and no shift notifications. Nothing reaches staff until you publish the shifts. Two costs sit outside the roster. Deputy's pricing FAQ states that administrators and supervisors count as billable users like anyone else. It also puts SMS at 160 characters a segment — 1c in the US, 5p in the UK, 5c in Australia, 7c elsewhere — while noting that many messages sent through Deputy run longer than one segment.

Frequently asked questions

Can you export your data from Deputy?

Exit check 2026-10-24; route excerpt: People > Add People > Bulk import and update > Update team members > Download …; came along (excerpts): Both team members with name, email, location, role, our Payroll ID, hired date and a Deputy ID / 29 columns, the same as the import template; lost (excerpts): The base rates (20 and 25) we pasted in on the first import: empty in the export and no pay rate in People; re-import: tested.

Deputy AI is listed as free on every plan. Is it?

For now, and the page says so in the same breath. Read on 19 September 2026, all three plan cards carry Deputy AI with the label INTRODUCTORY FREE and, directly underneath, the sentence that price and availability may change. That is more honest than most vendors manage, and it is also a clear instruction: do not choose Deputy for the AI, and do not build a process on a feature whose price has not been set. Pick your plan on the things you are actually paying for — scheduling, time clocking and labour-law compliance on Lite, auto-scheduling, demand forecasting and labour budgets on Core, SSO and access control on Pro — and treat anything AI-labelled as a bonus that may one day carry a per-user rate. If it becomes central to how you roster, ask your account manager for the post-introductory price in writing before you renew.

What does the annual plan actually lock me into?

Twelve months, paid upfront, with the size of your team fixed at the top end only. Deputy's own billing FAQ, read 19 September 2026, is unusually plain about it: on an annual plan you are charged immediately for the year on the day you upgrade, you can add users during the term — pro rata if you pre-pay — but you cannot change the plan you are on and you cannot lower the number of employees on the account. You may cancel before the twelve months are up, and there are no refunds if you do. Monthly billing is the opposite: you can change plan at any time without penalty. So the annual discount is roughly ten percent, and what it costs you is the ability to shrink. For a seasonal business — hospitality over winter, retail after Christmas — that is the wrong way round, and the ten percent is cheap to give up. For a stable headcount it is straightforwardly worth taking.

What will show up on the bill beyond the per-user price?

Three things, and none of them are in the plan cards. First, part-month users are full-month users: the FAQ states that a person added five days before month end is billed for the whole month, that archiving someone partway through bills the full month too, and that unarchiving bills a full month again regardless of the day. In a business with turnover, the same seat can be paid for twice in one month. Second, SMS is metered separately, per 160 characters — a cent in the US, five pence in the UK, five cents in Australia, seven cents elsewhere — with the page's own note that many Deputy messages run past 160 characters, so a shift-notification habit has a per-message cost attached. Third, the monthly minimum of $30, £20 or AU$30 an invoice applies whatever your headcount. Price a busy month with real turnover, not a quiet one with a stable roster.

Can I try Deputy without entering a payment card?

The homepage says so: read on 26 October 2026, deputy.com advertises a trial of up to 31 days with no credit card required. The words 'up to' matter. The page does not say which length applies to which region or plan, so the exact trial length you get and what happens to your data when it ends are not confirmed. The billing rules quoted above (part-month users charged as full months, admins counted as paid users, the minimum spend) apply from the moment you convert.
