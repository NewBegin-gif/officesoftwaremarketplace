There is no such thing as the Employment Hero price, and the first thing to fix is the currency: the pricing page is the Australian one, its disclaimer says every figure is AUD excluding GST, and there is no US page.

Best for

Australian and New Zealand employers who want HR and payroll from one vendor and will genuinely use both, where the per-employee rate beats separate systems.

Skip it if

you are budgeting in US dollars — the page is Australian, in AUD excluding GST, with no US equivalent — or you only need one of the modules, since each is sold separately and priced as though you are buying the platform.

Full pricing notes (3 more paragraphs)

Interview credits reset monthly, unused credits do not roll over, and overage is billed automatically with no hard cap. No HR or payroll subscription is required for Recruitment. HR Essentials A$10 per employee per month, HR Engage A$14; HR Elite and Employment Unlimited are quote-only. There is no standalone payroll tier on the page any more: payroll sits inside Employment Unlimited, and Managed Payroll is an add-on at A$20 per employee per month with a A$400 monthly minimum.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Then the structure. HR, recruitment, chat and payroll are separate subscriptions, most of them per employee, with a stated minimum of ten users underneath — so a six-person company is billed for ten. Payroll no longer carries its own price at all: it is inside the quote-only Employment Unlimited tier, or an A$20-a-head add-on with a A$400 floor. And the recruitment tiers meter AI video interviews rather than people, with credits that reset monthly, do not roll over, and overage billed automatically with no hard cap.

When the paid plan is worth it

This is a menu rather than a ladder, so the question is which modules you are buying — and in which currency. The page is the Australian one, its disclaimer says every figure is AUD excluding GST, and there is no US page. HR Essentials is A$10 per employee a month and HR Engage A$14; above them, HR Elite and Employment Unlimited are quote-only. Payroll no longer carries a price of its own: it sits inside Employment Unlimited, or you buy Managed Payroll at A$20 per employee with a A$400 monthly floor.

Recruitment is metered on a different axis and is the part worth doing sums on. A$199, A$359 and A$639 buy 50, 100 and 200 AI video interviews — A$3.98, A$3.59 and A$3.20 each, so the rate per interview keeps improving all the way up the published ladder. A 300-interview plan exists too, but Employment Hero prints no price for it, so there is no fourth figure to compare. Those are annual-billing rates; monthly billing is A$249 for the 50.

What upgrading does not solve: credits reset every month, unused ones do not roll over, and overage is billed automatically with no hard cap, so a heavy hiring month costs extra on the largest plan too. The ten-user minimum sits under the per-employee rates whichever tier you pick.

How to actually use Employment Hero

Build the sum yourself, and build it in Australian dollars: the pricing page is the AU one and every figure on it excludes GST. HR Essentials is A$10 a head, HR Engage A$14, Chat A$2 with a A$20 monthly minimum — and a stated ten-user minimum sits under all of it, so a six-person company is billed for ten. Payroll has no standalone price any more: budget Managed Payroll at A$20 a head with a A$400 floor, or get a quote for Employment Unlimited.

Treat recruitment as a separate decision, because it is metered differently from everything else. It counts AI video interviews rather than people — A$199, A$359 and A$639 for 50, 100 and 200 on annual billing, or A$249 for the 50 if you pay monthly. A 300-interview plan is named in the disclaimer with no price attached, so ask for that one rather than budgeting a guess. Check the overage before you commit: credits reset monthly, do not roll over, and the excess is billed automatically with no cap.

Read the asterisks before you compare this with anything: they are the difference between the quote you modelled and the invoice you get.

Frequently asked questions

Who counts towards the per-employee bill?

More people than the word employee suggests. Employment Hero's invoice guide (read 6 October 2026) bills the HR platform on the higher of the users in your contract and the actual users in the system, and an actual user is anyone set up in it who is active or pending at the end of the month: employees, contractors, managers, admins and the CEO all count. The count is a snapshot taken on the last day of the month, so someone offboarded on the 1st is billed for the whole month before. The pricing page adds a floor of 10 users on every HR plan, and each add-on bills the greater of its monthly minimum and your actual billable employees. Billing runs monthly in arrears: the invoice at the start of a month covers the month before.

Can I cancel or downsize online?

You can ask online, but you cannot finish it yourself. The billing article (read 6 October 2026) sends plan changes, add-on removal, more or fewer users and cancellation through the same request form under Billings > Subscriptions, available to admins: you pick a reason, describe the change and submit, and an Employment Hero representative contacts you to carry it out. Neither that article nor the pricing page states a notice period or minimum term, and the pricing page refers to a customer agreement for the contracted number of users, so read that agreement before signing: not confirmed on the public pages.

What should I weigh before buying HR & People software?

Compliance is country by country, so the honest answer is: only where the vendor says it is, and only for the entity type you actually have. Ask which countries are covered by its own entities versus a local partner, who carries the liability if something is filed wrong, and how changes in local law reach you. Get those answers in the contract rather than the sales deck.
