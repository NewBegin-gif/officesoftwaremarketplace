Cloudways Review (2026)

IT & ProductivityResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-01

"Fully managed" here means the server, not your site — and that distinction is where the money is.

Best for: agencies and PHP shops running many sites who want servers managed but not metered. One server holds as many sites as it can carry with no visit caps, billing is hourly and post-paid with no annual term, and Magento and Laravel are first-class alongside WordPress. It suits teams who genuinely do not want anyone patching kernels at 2am.
Who should skip it: if you are comfortable in a terminal, or you run a single small site. Going direct to the cloud provider with your own control panel keeps the server in your own account for materially less. And one low-traffic site is better served by shared hosting, or by a per-site managed host that bundles CDN and application support. Cloudways also has no email hosting of its own, reselling a Rackspace add-on instead.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Cloudways' own support page puts infrastructure and platform work in the included tier but treats application help as guidance only. Plugin and theme troubleshooting, database work and performance tuning sit behind Advanced Support at $100/month or 10% of your invoice, whichever is higher, on a six-month minimum. On a small server that add-on costs more than the server it supports. Content changes, custom code debugging and code audits stay out of scope on every tier, paid ones included.

The deeper catch is ownership: Cloudways documents that you get no root access and cannot use your own cloud credentials, so the machine sits in Cloudways' account — you cannot fire the management layer and keep the server, you migrate. And read the billing terms before you rely on it: their help centre states that an unpaid invoice restricts server access and can lead to the server and its backups being deleted, with backups kept for only 14 days after deletion.

What Cloudways does

Managed-hosting layer that rents you servers on DigitalOcean, Vultr, Linode, AWS or Google Cloud through one dashboard, with the stack, caching, staging, cloning, backups and 24/7 platform support pre-built on top. You buy a server and put as many sites on it as it holds — no per-site plans and no visit metering — billed hourly and invoiced monthly in arrears with no contract. The trade is control: Cloudways' own documentation states there is no root access and no bring-your-own cloud account, so the server lives in Cloudways' name. Owned by DigitalOcean since September 2022.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is no free tier here — only a 3-day trial that needs no credit card — so the question is not free versus paid but which add-ons you bolt onto a server that starts at $14/month. The boundary that decides money is support scope. The free Standard tier covers infrastructure and promises an initial ticket response within 12 hours, or 3 hours for high priority tickets; plugin and theme troubleshooting, performance and database optimisation and server error investigation only start at Advanced Support.

Proactive monitoring is not that clean a line: the comparison table gives Standard "Alerts" and both Advanced and Premium "Proactive Investigation". Advanced carries a list price of $100/month but was displayed at $25/month on 31 July 2026 under a "75% OFF for lifetime" label, or 10% of your invoice, whichever is higher. Premium is $500/month on the same 10% rule, and both require a minimum commitment of six months. Advanced also tightens the ticket SLA to 6 hours, or 30 minutes for high priority.

Upgrading fixes none of the hard limits: website content changes, debugging custom code, auditing your code and migrating away from Cloudways stay out of scope on every tier, Premium included.

How to actually use Cloudways

Signup needs no card and the trial runs three days; the pricing page lets you pick a cloud provider (DigitalOcean, Vultr, Linode, AWS, GCE) and a server size before signing up. The migration add-on page shows two tables that do not agree. One table lists WordPress/WooCommerce as "Free Unlimited Sites" with Joomla/OpenCart, LMS & Ecommerce and custom applications each at $99 per application; the other charges $25 per application for WordPress/Woo, $99 for Joomla/OpenCart, $125 for LMS Platforms and $199 for custom applications.

The page does not say which customers each table applies to. Cloudways writes that "Your first migration is included at no cost", that migrations are usually completed within 1–2 business days depending on the complexity of your site, and that "WordPress users can also migrate unlimited sites at no cost using our WordPress Migrator Plugin". A 16 June 2026 changelog entry gives Cloudways Copilot automatic 5xx monitoring of hosted WordPress and WooCommerce websites.

The natural comparison is Going direct on DigitalOcean with your own control panel — DigitalOcean owns Cloudways and sells both sides, which makes this the honest comparison: the same 8GB machine is $48/month as a droplet and $99/month wrapped in Cloudways. Add DigitalOcean's own daily backups and a control-panel licence and a like-for-like self-managed box lands nearer $75-80, so the real premium is smaller than the sticker gap suggests. The decisive difference is not price but ownership: with your own droplet the account and root are yours and you can drop the panel and keep the server, whereas leaving Cloudways is a migration.
