Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 29 Sep 2026

Basic $59/month billed annually ($79 month-to-month, advertised as 'save $20/mo') for ONE user, with no GPS tracking and no QuickBooks Online sync; Essentials $149/month ($189) for five users; MAX $299/month ($329) for eight users, sold through a demo booking. Extra users on MAX are $35/month each; the page no longer states an extra-seat price for Essentials (on 2026-09-04 it read $100 on Essentials and $75 on MAX).

On 2026-09-29 the cards also showed introductory offers on monthly billing: $26 for the first month on Basic and Essentials, and $99 a month for three months on MAX when booked through a demo. There is a 14-day free trial and no credit card is required. Card processing is quoted as 'as low as' 2.59% and bank payments carry a separate 1% fee, so the sticker is the smaller half of the bill: on $40,000 a month of card revenue the processing alone is at least $1,036. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

When the paid plan is worth it

The subscription is not the biggest line on this bill. Card processing is quoted as 'as low as' 2.59% — Housecall Pro's own words, so treat it as a floor rather than your rate — and on $40,000 of monthly card revenue that is at least $1,036, more than three times the $299 MAX plan. Bank payments carry a separate 1% fee. Within the subscription the trap is seats, not tiers.

Basic is $59 a month billed annually ($79 monthly) and covers one user, with no GPS tracking and no QuickBooks sync — two of the things a field business buys this for. Essentials is $149 ($189) for five users and MAX $299 ($329) for eight. The seats beyond those counts are where it bites, and the page moved on them: on 29 September 2026 extra users on MAX read $35 a month each (down from $75 on 4 September), while the $100 Essentials extra-seat rate we read then is no longer stated.

For a crew of six to eight that flips the arithmetic: MAX includes eight users for $299, less than the $349 that Essentials plus two seats at the old rate came to, so ask what a sixth Essentials seat costs before assuming Essentials is the cheaper plan. What moving up a tier does not fix is the processing rate. It is the same percentage on Basic as on MAX, so the tier you choose changes the smaller number on your bill and leaves the larger one exactly where it was.

How to actually use Housecall Pro

Price the card processing before the subscription, because it is the larger number. Housecall Pro says rates are 'as low as' 2.59% and that they 'start at' 2.59%, so that is a floor and not a quote — ask what you would actually get against your own monthly card volume and average ticket, and get it in writing. Bank payments are charged 1% separately. Then count seats before you compare tiers.

Basic covers one user and omits GPS tracking and QuickBooks sync; Essentials covers five at $149 and MAX eight at $299; users beyond eight cost $35 a month each on MAX, and the page no longer states what a sixth Essentials user costs, so get that figure before you compare. For most crews that arithmetic moves the decision further than the feature list does. Use the 14-day trial on a live job rather than a demo one.

Book it, dispatch it, take the payment and send the invoice — the whole loop, on a phone, from a van if that is where your team works.

Frequently asked questions

What does Housecall Pro cost?

$59 a month for Basic (one user), $149 for Essentials (five users) and $299 for MAX (eight users), all billed annually; month-to-month those are $79, $189 and $329. The trial runs 14 days and asks for no card. Read on 4 September 2026 and unchanged on 29 September 2026.

What does it really cost for a crew of seven?

On the published figures, $299 a month before payments. MAX is $299 billed annually and includes eight users, and on 29 September 2026 extra users beyond eight read $35 a month each. Essentials is $149 for five, but the page no longer states what a sixth and seventh user cost there (it read $100 a month each on 4 September 2026, which made it $349), so ask before you pick the cheaper-looking tier. On top of that, card processing is quoted as 'as low as' 2.59%, so a business taking $40,000 a month on cards pays at least $1,000 in processing — the number that should drive the comparison.

What is missing from the cheapest plan?

GPS tracking and the QuickBooks Online sync, both of which arrive on Essentials. For a solo operator that may not matter; for anyone dispatching vehicles or doing their own books, Basic is not the plan even though it looks like the entry point.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
