A quote appears here only after it matched the live page word for word on the date shown. “Being verified” means we have not matched it yet, not that it does not exist.

The 1 October price increase the page announced in September has happened. The standard monthly prices are now Early $27 (was $25), Growing $59 (was $55) and Established $97 (was $90). The headline offer has changed too: 'Get 80% off your plan for your first 3 months', so Early is $5.40 a month for three months and then $27, Growing $11.80 and then $59, Established $19.40 and then $97; a second button offers one month free instead.

Early still lists 20 invoices and 5 bills a month, with a footnote marker on the invoice limit; on our 19 September reading that footnote said the limit counts both approving and sending and that transactions initiated by app partners may count toward it. New on Early: auto-reconcile is an optional add-on at $5.00 a month (included on Growing and Established). Growing allows 3 budgets.

Multi-currency, project tracking, expense and mileage claims and international bill payments remain on Established only. Optional add-ons: Inventory Plus (no price shown, not on Early) and Xero Payroll powered by Gusto at $36 a month plus $6 per employee or contractor. Standard ACH bill payments are free; fees apply for other methods and for cross-border payments. Subscriptions auto-renew monthly until cancelled. The offer terms were not re-read on this date; on 19 September they limited the then-current 90% offer to new US customers buying their first organisation and to the base subscription only, prices excluded tax and a downgrade was only allowed one month after an upgrade. Xero prices differently by country. Plans change, so check the live price on their site before you buy.

What buyers report about Xero

Hands-on: not tested by usReported by buyers: G2, Capterra, GetApp and Trustpilot, read August 2026

Themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Xero ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Unlimited users on every tier, including the cheapest: a real advantage over per-seat competitors.
  • • Non-accountants become productive in hours rather than days. Ease of use scores 9.0/10 on G2 against 8.2 for QuickBooks Online. 4.4 on both G2 and Capterra across 15,311 total reviews.
What they complain about
  • • 46% of reviewers feel negatively about cost, and add-ons for advanced features push it out of reach for smaller teams. Established ran $90 a month when these reviews were read; since 1 October 2026 it is $97.
  • • The forced switch from classic to new invoicing drew widespread complaints about lost functionality and a clunkier interface.
  • • Manual payroll entry was removed in some regions: check yours before you migrate.
  • • Support response times are a consistent complaint, alongside repeated price increases.

Where the ratings disagree. 4.4 on G2 and Capterra against 3.7 on Trustpilot. Narrower than most in this list, which itself says something: the complaints here are about price and product decisions, not about billing conduct.

Read the second number

Every headline price on Xero's US page is a promotion: 80% off for the first three months. Early is $5.40 a month for three months and then $27. Growing is $11.80 and then $59. Established is $19.40 and then $97. Those standard rates are new: on 1 October 2026 Xero raised them from $25, $55 and $90, as the page had announced in September. Payroll powered by Gusto is an add-on at $36 a month plus $6 per employee or contractor.

So read the second number, not the first. It is printed right there, but smaller, and the plan people choose on the strength of $5.40 a month is the one that becomes $27, five times as much. Work out what the offer is worth across a year, because '80% off' sounds larger than it is. Three months at 80% off is 2.4 months of free service, a 20% discount on your first year and nothing after that.

In cash: Early costs $259.20 in year one against $324 at the standard rate, Growing $566.40 against $708, Established $931.20 against $1,164. Budget from the standard rate and treat the promotion as a one-off rebate. Two more things before you commit. Check which country's page you are looking at, because Xero prices and tiers differ by market. And be clear that the entry tier is a genuine floor rather than a taster: multi-currency and project tracking are on Established at $97, not on Early at $27.

The decision that matters with accounting software is not the monthly fee: your books end up inside it, which is what makes leaving expensive a year later. Pick on whether your accountant works in it and whether it connects to your bank properly, and let the $27-versus-$59 question follow from that.

How to actually use Xero

Check which country's page you are on before you read a single price. Xero's tiers and rates differ by market, so a comparison you found elsewhere may describe a different product at a different price, and the US figures are not the UK or Australian ones. Then budget the standard rate rather than the promotional one. Every headline is 80% off for three months: $5.40 becomes $27, $11.80 becomes $59, $19.40 becomes $97.

Across a full year the offer is worth about 20%, so plan from month four. The standard rates themselves rose on 1 October 2026. Pick the tier on the features that force it rather than on price. Multi-currency and project tracking sit on Established at $97, and auto-reconcile costs $5 a month extra on Early. Inventory Plus is a separate add-on again. Before any of that, ask your accountant or bookkeeper which package they work in, and connect your bank feed on day one.

Accounting software is chosen once and lived in for years, and the two things that decide whether it works are whether your bank feed is reliable and whether the person doing your year-end is fluent in it. The monthly fee is the smallest part of this decision.

Frequently asked questions

Did Xero raise its prices in October 2026?

Yes. On 9 October 2026 the US page showed Early at $27 a month, Growing at $59 and Established at $97, up from $25, $55 and $90 on our September readings. The page had announced an increase from 1 October 2026.

What is the current Xero promotion worth?

Read on 9 October 2026: 80% off the first three months, so Early is $5.40, Growing $11.80 and Established $19.40 a month for three months. Across the first year that is a 20% discount: Early costs $259.20 instead of $324. After that the standard rate applies, and subscriptions auto-renew monthly.

Does the $27 Early plan let me invoice normally?

Only up to a limit. Early is capped at 20 invoices and 5 bills a month. On our 19 September reading the footnote said the invoice limit counts both approving and sending, and that transactions started by connected apps may count toward it. Growing at $59 has no such cap in the comparison table, which makes it the realistic entry price for anyone whose billing runs through connected apps.

What else lands on the bill besides the subscription?

Payroll powered by Gusto is $36 a month plus $6 per employee or contractor. Auto-reconcile is $5 a month extra on Early. Inventory Plus is optional on Growing and Established, not available on Early, and has no price on the page. Standard ACH bill payments are free; other methods and cross-border payments carry fees.

When are Payroll, Projects, Expenses and bank-feed charges billed?

In arrears. The FAQ on xero.com/us/pricing-plans (read 5 November 2026) says that usage charges for Xero Payroll, Xero Projects or Xero Expenses, and a chargeable direct bank feed, "will be invoiced for the previous month". The invoice arrives at the end of your monthly billing period and is paid on the date shown on it. So the first month you switch on payroll, the cost shows up on the following invoice, not at sign-up. The same page notes that subscriptions renew monthly until cancelled and that a move to a cheaper plan is possible one month after upgrading.
