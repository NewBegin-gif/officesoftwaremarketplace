Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 31 Aug 2026

(2026) Four plans, each quoted both yearly and monthly: Starter $97/yr or $9/mo (30 digital scans/mo, 20 paper scans/yr, 1 Magic Envelope/yr, 1 user, 3 years of scan storage); Pro $297/yr or $29/mo (200 digital scans/mo, 20 paper scans/mo, 1 Magic Envelope per quarter, Gmail receipt sync, QuickBooks Online/Xero, 3 users); Plus $797/yr or $79/mo (750 digital scans/mo, 50 paper scans/mo, 1 Magic Envelope/mo, 10 users); Paper Plus $1,997/yr or $179/mo (500 digital scans/mo, 200 paper scans/mo, 2 envelopes/mo).

Overages by tier: $0.30/$0.15/$0.10 per digital scan, $2.00 or $1.50 per paper scan, $9 per extra envelope. Unlimited mileage tracking on every plan. A 30-day money-back guarantee rather than a free trial — the card is charged at signup, and the vendor asks you not to claim the refund once a Magic Envelope has shipped. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

That trick is also the meter to watch: envelope frequency defines the tiers, and heavy paper businesses land in the $179/mo bracket quickly. If your receipts are already digital, cheaper scan-only apps exist; Shoeboxed earns its price precisely when physical paper is the problem.

What Shoeboxed does

Turn paper and digital receipts into organized, IRS-ready expense data with OCR plus human verification — syncs with QuickBooks and Xero.

What buyers report about Shoeboxed

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Shoeboxed ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • The Magic Envelope is the reason people choose it specifically: post a full envelope of receipts and it is all in your account a few days later, ready for the accountant. Nothing else does that. 88% satisfaction across 259 reviews.
  • • For a business with a shoebox rather than a workflow, it removes the whole problem.
What they complain about
  • • Data extraction is not always accurate, categorisation is clunky and bulk actions are limited — reviewers report spending time correcting rather than filing.
  • • Automatic categorisation cannot be customised to match your chart of accounts.
  • • No automatic GPS mileage tracking; it is manual entry, unlike competing apps.
  • • $23/month annually or $29 monthly, with overage fees on top. For a low receipt volume reviewers say it does not pay.

The guarantee ends the moment the envelope ships

Every plan is quoted both ways, yearly and monthly, so put them side by side. Starter is $97 a year or $9 a month. Pro is $297 or $29. Plus is $797 or $79. Paper Plus is $1,997 or $179. Paying yearly saves 11% on Starter, 17% on Pro and 19% on Plus — the discount grows as you climb, which is unusual and worth taking if you are staying.

On the scan meter Pro is the value tier and it is not close: 2,400 scans a year for $297 is $0.124 each, against Starter's 360 for $97, or $0.269. Plus is better still at $0.089. Pro is also where the accounting sync arrives — QuickBooks Online and Xero — which for anyone reconciling receipts against books is the feature that makes the tool worth having at all. But scans are not what the tiers really meter.

The Magic Envelope is: you post a stuffed envelope of paper receipts and humans digitise them. Starter gets one a year, Pro one a quarter, Plus one a month and Paper Plus two. If you are mailing paper every month the ladder is decided for you at Plus and above, and the $797-a-year bracket arrives faster than you expect. Then read the guarantee before you test it, because it is not a free trial.

Your card is charged at signup and you have 30 days to ask for the money back — except that the vendor asks you not to request a refund once it has shipped you a Magic Envelope, since it has already covered the mailing and processing. So the one thing that proves whether this tool is worth buying is the thing that quietly ends your right to a refund. That is the trade to weigh: if your receipts are already PDFs and email confirmations, cheaper scan-only apps do the job and the envelope is a feature you are subsidising.

If the problem is a physical shoebox of paper — a trades business, a fleet, anyone collecting fuel and parts receipts in a van — this is precisely what it is for.

How to actually use Shoeboxed

Test the digital side inside the 30-day window and send the envelope only once you have decided. This is a money-back guarantee, not a free trial: your card is charged at signup, and the vendor asks you not to request a refund once it has shipped you a Magic Envelope, because it has already paid for the mailing and processing. So start with the phone app and your genuinely awkward paper — faded thermal receipts, handwritten invoices, a crumpled fuel docket — rather than clean printouts, and check what comes back for accuracy on the fields you actually use: date, vendor, total, tax.

Count envelopes rather than scans when you pick a tier, because envelope frequency is what separates the plans: one a year on Starter, one a quarter on Pro, one a month on Plus and two on Paper Plus. Check the overage rates while you are there, since that is where a busy month lands: $0.30, $0.15 or $0.10 per digital scan depending on tier, $2.00 or $1.50 per paper scan, and $9 for an extra envelope.

And if your receipts are already digital, stop here — cheaper scan-only tools do that job. The value of this one is the paper, plus the mileage tracking that every plan includes.
