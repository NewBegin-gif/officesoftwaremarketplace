What buyers report about doola

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run doola ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • For a non-US founder it removes the parts that are genuinely hard — EIN, US address, bank introduction — and reviewers rate the hand-holding highly. 4.7 on Trustpilot across 1,000+ reviews.
  • • Support is reachable on chat, email, phone and WhatsApp, which matters across time zones.
What they complain about
  • • $297 a year, every year, not once. Against one-time formation plus a registered agent at $39-$125 a year, the subscription costs more over any multi-year horizon. Do that arithmetic before signing.
  • • 1-star BBB rating and not accredited as of 2026, with complaints about billing, refunds and support response on the lower tier.
  • • The 60-day guarantee covers doola's fee only. State fees are gone, and refunds generally stop once your filing is submitted.
  • • If Mercury rejects your bank application, the plan is not refunded and there is no second bank partner.

Where the ratings disagree. 4.7 on Trustpilot against 1 star at the BBB. Trustpilot captures the formation experience, which people mostly like; the BBB captures what happens when someone wants their money back.

When the paid plan is worth it

doola's entry point, Starter at $297/year plus state fees, covers LLC formation, EIN application, a registered agent, and a US address — enough for a solo founder who already handles bookkeeping and taxes elsewhere. The concrete push toward paying more is tax filing: Starter explicitly excludes federal and state tax filing and bookkeeping tools, so anyone who needs those must jump to Tax and Compliance at $1,999/year. A dedicated bookkeeper and monthly financial statements are gated further, at Business-in-a-Box, $2,999/year or $329/month.

What no tier changes: EIN turnaround is set by the IRS, not by doola's plan — without a US Social Security Number, doola's own materials cite 4-6 weeks, extending to 8-10 weeks during tax season, regardless of which package you buy. State filing fees are also billed separately from every tier, so upgrading doesn't roll those into the subscription price.

How to actually use doola

Getting started means creating a doola account and submitting company name, state, member details, address, phone, and email — a step doola describes as taking about 15 minutes with no documents required up front. doola then files the LLC, averaging about a week depending on state. The formation documents needed for banking and tax purposes become available in 1-2 business days for US residents. Opening a US bank account through doola's partner portal then takes another 3-5 business days, requiring a passport for international founders or a government ID for US residents.

The recurring bottleneck reported is the EIN for founders without a US Social Security Number: doola's own guidance cites 4-6 weeks, extending to 8-10 weeks during tax season, since that step depends on IRS processing rather than doola's own speed. Until the EIN arrives, both bank account approval and tax filing stay blocked.

Frequently asked questions

What does it cost to form a US company with doola?

$297 for the first year on Starter, plus your state's filing fee, which runs between $50 and $500 depending on the state. That covers formation, EIN, registered agent and a US address, and renews at $300 a year. Read on 17 August 2026.

What are the bigger packages for?

Tax and Compliance at $1,999 a year and Business-in-a-Box at $2,999 add filings, compliance and bookkeeping support rather than more formation. Both were discounted 25% when we read the page on 24 August 2026 — to $1,499 and $2,249, part of an up-to-$750-off sale that doola dates to 31 August — so check whether that offer is still running before working from the list price.

What is not included?

State fees, always, and they are the part that varies most by where you incorporate. Budget $50 to $500 on top of whichever package you take, and note that the renewal ($300 on Starter) is slightly above the first-year price rather than below it.

Is doola safe to run my finances through?

doola isn't a bank and it doesn't hold or move your money. It forms the entity, acts as registered agent and keeps the filings and books straight; where it helps you open a US business account, the account and the balance sit with the partner bank, not with doola. So judge it on filing accuracy and accountability rather than custody: who is registered agent of record, what happens — and who pays — if a filing or return is late or wrong, how your EIN and identity documents are stored, and whether you can move your entity and records elsewhere without a fight. Get those answers in writing before you commit to a multi-year bundle.
