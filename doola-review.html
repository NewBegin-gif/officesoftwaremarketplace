doola: Formation Is Cheap, the Compliance Tier Is Not

Forming a US company through doola costs $297 a year. Filing its taxes costs $1,999. That gap is the whole decision, and it is not the gap most founders are looking at when they sign up.

What it does

doola forms a US LLC or C-Corp for you, applies for the EIN, acts as registered agent and gives you a US address. For a founder outside the United States that bundle removes a genuine obstacle: the paperwork assumes you already have a US footprint, and doola is the footprint.

The pricing, and the number that is not doola's

Read on doola.com/pricing on 1 September 2026. Starter is $297 a year plus state fees and covers formation, EIN, registered agent and address. Pulse bookkeeping runs a 30-day trial and then renews at $300 a year. Tax and Compliance, the tier the page marks Most Popular, is $1,999 a year plus state fees. Business-in-a-Box is $2,999 a year, or $329 a month, also plus state fees. doola's own FAQ confirms every plan is billed annually.

Those state fees are the number people miss. They run roughly $50 to $500 depending on which state you register in, they are charged by the state and not by doola, and they arrive on top of every tier. A Wyoming LLC and a Delaware C-Corp are not the same bill.

One timing note, because it was true last week and is not true today: through 31 August doola ran an automatic discount of up to $750 that could not be combined with anything else, showing $222, $1,499 and $2,249 for the three tiers. That window closed yesterday, which is exactly why the reader code above applies again from today.

The wait nobody prices in

Every tier promises the same thing about speed, and none of them can deliver it. Your EIN — the tax number without which you cannot open a bank account or file anything — is issued by the IRS, not by doola. For a founder without a US Social Security Number, doola's own guidance says four to six weeks, stretching to eight to ten during tax season. No plan changes that number, because no plan changes the IRS.

This matters more than the price comparison, because everything else waits on it. The formation filing itself averages about a week. Documents you need for banking appear in one to two business days. A US bank account through doola's partner portal takes another three to five, and international founders need a passport for that step. But until the EIN lands, both the bank account and any filing stay blocked. Plan your launch around ten weeks, not ten days, and you will not be disappointed.

What each tier actually buys

Starter, $297 a year plus state fees. Formation of an LLC or C-Corp, the EIN application, a registered agent and a US address. It renews at $300. For a solo founder who already has a bookkeeper, or who does the books themselves, this is the whole product.

Tax and Compliance, $1,999 a year plus state fees. The page marks it Most Popular, and the reason people land here is specific: Starter explicitly excludes federal and state tax filing and bookkeeping tools. There is no tier between the two. So the first filing season is when most founders discover they are on the wrong plan, and the correction costs 6.7 times what they were paying.

Business-in-a-Box, $2,999 a year or $329 a month, plus state fees. Adds a dedicated bookkeeper and monthly financial statements. Worth it when the hours you spend on the books cost more than the difference; not before.

Pulse, the bookkeeping automation, sits alongside these with a 30-day trial and renews at $300 a year.

The line item that is not doola's

State filing fees run roughly $50 to $500 depending on where you incorporate, they are charged by the state, and they sit on top of every tier — upgrading does not roll them in. Two founders on the same $297 plan can pay a $450 difference purely on the state they picked. Choose the state before you choose the plan; it moves the bill more than the tier does.

Who it is for

Best for founders outside the United States who want formation, EIN and compliance handled in one place. The paperwork assumes you already have a US footprint; doola is the footprint, and that is worth real money when you do not have one.

Skip it if you are a US resident comfortable filing yourself. The state fee plus a cheap registered agent costs a fraction of the bundle, and you were paying the state fee either way. And once you are established, a local accountant is often cheaper than the $1,999 tier for the same filings.

How we would start

Pick the state first. Then start on Starter and leave your bookkeeping where it already lives — Tax and Compliance is far easier to justify once you have a filing season to point at than on day one, and moving later costs nothing but an upgrade. Set the state fee aside in your own records as a separate line, because it will not appear on doola's renewal and it will surprise you next year otherwise.

Submit the formation details — company name, state, members, address, phone, email — which takes about fifteen minutes and needs no documents up front. Then start the EIN clock and plan everything downstream around it.
