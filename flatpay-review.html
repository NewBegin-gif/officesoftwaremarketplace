Flatpay Review (2026)

Financial OperationsResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-01

“Zero monthly fees” is the headline, and there genuinely is no subscription — but there is a floor.

Best for: a physical shop, café or bar with steady card turnover above £995 a month that wants one predictable rate on every card type instead of a pricing table full of card categories. The on-site installation and 24/7 support suit an owner who would rather not configure hardware, and daily payouts help if cash flow is tight.
Who should skip it: if your card takings are small or seasonal: the £995 monthly minimum and the £39 shortfall charge punish exactly that pattern, and the price list says the assessment is annual so a quiet winter still counts. Skip it too if you are online-only — this is built around physical terminals and an in-person sales visit — or if you turn over more than £200,000 a year on cards, because then every published price is replaced by a quote.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 20 Aug 2026

(2026-08-13, read off flatpay.com/en-gb/pricing and their separate /pricelist page) Flatpay charges one rate per transaction on every card type, with no monthly subscription. The card terminal is 1.49% and costs £0 to start. The three point-of-sale tiers all run at 1.69% and differ only in what you pay up front for onboarding and installation: £995 for mobile POS, £1,495 for POS Pro and £2,495 for POS Premium.

Every one of those four prices applies only below £200,000 in annual card turnover; above it, all four say “custom rate — contact sales”, so the published ladder is for smaller merchants only. Included on all plans: 24/7 support, daily payouts and a customer portal. The part that needs reading is the price list, which is linked from the footer rather than from the pricing page.

It sets a **minimum monthly turnover of £995** and charges **£39 in any month you fall short** — assessed annually, so seasonal businesses are explicitly in scope. It also lists £410 for a replacement terminal, £25 to re-onboard after a change in business registration, and hardware separately: tablet £495, printer £495, cash drawer £200. All of those figures exclude VAT. One inconsistency worth raising with their salesperson: the pricing page puts POS Pro onboarding at £1,495 while the price list quotes a “Pro POS bundle” at £1,995 — a £500 gap between two pages on the same site. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Miss £995 of card turnover in a month and you are charged £39, which for a quiet month is the same thing as a monthly fee wearing a different hat. Do the sum before you sign: a shop turning over £500 on cards pays £7.45 in transaction fees and then £39 on top, so the cost of taking cards is six times what the rate suggests. The rate itself is straightforward and the terminal really is free to start, so this is a good fit for a busy till and a poor one for a market stall, a seasonal business or anyone whose card volume swings.

And the four-figure POS installation charges are real money that the “no hidden fees” promise sits directly above — they are disclosed, but they are not what most people picture when they read that line.

What Flatpay does

Card terminals, POS systems and online payments for physical shops across Europe, sold with on-site installation and 24/7 support. One flat rate on every card type: 1.49% on the terminal, 1.69% on the POS tiers, with daily payouts and no monthly subscription. Read their separate price list before you sign, though: it carries a £995 minimum monthly turnover with a £39 charge when you miss it, £410 for a replacement terminal, and POS installation from £995 to £2,495 — all excluding VAT (read 13 Aug 2026).

What buyers report about Flatpay

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Flatpay ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Positive reviews are almost all about a named salesperson: merchants describe a rep who compared their previous card machine's rate line by line, set the terminal up, and kept checking in afterwards. The onboarding is a person, not a portal, and reviewers who liked it say so in those terms.
  • • The flat rate itself draws very little complaint. Where merchants are unhappy it is about the contract and the extras, not about the percentage they were quoted.
What they complain about
  • • The most detailed negative reviews say the term is 36 months and that cancelling means paying the remaining months, which reviewers say they did not understand they were signing. Flatpay's own marketing line is 'no contract', so read the agreement before the visit ends.
  • • Merchants also report a minimum monthly card turnover with a charge for falling short, described as not explained at signing. That matches the minimum on Flatpay's separate price list, which is linked from the footer rather than from the pricing page.
  • • One reviewer describes being cold-called and then asked to send passport, personal ID and home energy bills over email or WhatsApp during onboarding.
  • • A reviewer reports an invoice dated December 2025 arriving in August 2026, with Flatpay saying it could no longer access the underlying records.

Where the ratings disagree. The gap is not between platforms, it is between Flatpay's own two pages. The pricing page says 'Zero monthly fees' and 'No fine print or surprises'; the price list behind the footer link carries the turnover minimum and the shortfall charge, and the contract-length complaints on Trustpilot cluster on exactly that gap. Flatpay replies to roughly half its negative reviews, and its profile notes replies typically take over a month.

When the paid plan is worth it

The three point-of-sale tiers charge the identical transaction rate of 1.69%, so the £995, £1,495 and £2,495 you pay up front buy onboarding and installation rather than a better price. That £1,500 spread never earns itself back through the rate, because there is no rate difference to earn it back with -- decide it purely on how much setup help you want. The card terminal on its own is cheaper per transaction at 1.49% and costs nothing to start.

The cost that is easiest to miss sits on a page linked from the footer rather than from pricing: a minimum monthly turnover of £995, with £39 charged in any month you fall below it. It is assessed annually, so a seasonal business is explicitly in scope and a quiet quarter is three separate charges. Also on that page: £410 for a replacement terminal, £25 to re-onboard after a change in business registration, and hardware priced separately at £495 for a tablet, £495 for a printer and £200 for a cash drawer, all excluding VAT.

Two boundaries to settle in writing. Every published rate applies only below £200,000 in annual card turnover; above that all four read custom rate, so the ladder is for smaller merchants only. And the site contradicts itself on POS Pro onboarding: £1,495 on the pricing page against £1,995 on the price list, a £500 gap between two pages of the same site. Ask which one the contract will say.

How to actually use Flatpay

There is no sign-up button, and that shapes the whole start. Flatpay's own three steps are an in-person meeting with one of their POS experts, a tailored offer built around your setup and business type, and then full on-site installation of hardware and software, with training. So the first thing to prepare is a sales visit, and the figure to bring to it is your annual card turnover, because every published rate applies only below GBP 200,000 and above that all four products read custom rate.

Once you are live the helpdesk carries the self-service: POS quick start, mPOS and terminal setup, payouts, accounting integration and the customer portal.

Common Flatpay setup mistakes

Where Flatpay setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

The part teams underestimate is the paperwork tied to your company registration number.

  • Flatpay registers the agreement against that number and treats the two as inseparable, so a change of ownership means a new agreement rather than an amendment.
  • Changes to company details have to arrive as a written request by email, and an address change needs telling them well in advance.
