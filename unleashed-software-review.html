What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

A wholesaler running 1,500 orders with six users who wants phone support pays $399 for Core, $200 for the order tier, $207 for three extra seats and $99 for Standard support. That is $905 a month, or 2.3 times the headline, before a one-off onboarding fee of at least $449. Support being a paid add-on rather than part of the plan is the detail most comparison tables miss. Price the bundle you will actually be on: count your busiest month's orders, your real seat count, and decide whether you can live without phone support, then compare that total against the alternatives rather than comparing entry prices.

What they praise
  • • Reviewers say new staff can be trained on it in about two hours, which is unusual for inventory software at this level.
  • • The Xero connection is described as near-perfect and the single reason several reviewers picked it.
  • • Shopify syncing removes manual order entry, and the assembly calculation reports back to the storefront how many units can actually be built.

What they complain about
  • • A long-term user reports the Production module unreliable around BOMs and assemblies, needing manual intervention or workarounds.
  • • Several reviewers export to Excel because the built-in reports lack the options they need.
  • • Lag is mentioned by more than one reviewer, including on fast connections.
  • • One customer found their monthly subscription had gone up by $100 with no prior notice or authorisation; another was billed an extra month on cancellation despite the FAQ saying they could cancel at any time.
  • • When a long-term customer raised service problems, the response they describe was an offer to pay roughly $100 a month more for the Customer Success plan.
  • • The purchase-order screen does not show what is already on order, on hand, or recently sold, so reviewers do that arithmetic elsewhere.
  • • Named gaps include no demand forecasting, no way to track returnable items such as kegs, and no email logging against customer or supplier records.

Where the ratings disagree. The complaints line up with the pricing structure rather than contradicting it. When the paid plan is worth it

Compare the two bundles on the figures the pricing page actually publishes and the step looks expensive. Core is $399 a month for 3 user licences, 100 sales orders and 250,000 API calls. Pro is $729 for 5 licences, the same 100 sales orders, and 500,000 API calls. Since extra users cost $69 a month on Core, buying the two additional seats there brings you to $537 for five people, $192 a month less than Pro, and on the published lines the only thing that $192 buys is 250,000 more API calls.

If you are not making those calls, ask what else Pro includes before you take it, because the page does not say. The bigger point is that neither headline is the bill. Both bundles include just 100 sales orders a month, and orders past that are a separate line: $70 up to 500, $200 up to 1,500, $320 up to 3,000, $490 for unlimited. Support is also an add-on rather than an inclusion, at $99 a month for Standard or $239 for Premier.

A modest business doing 400 orders with standard support is therefore at $399 plus $70 plus $99, which is $568, forty per cent above the advertised price. Onboarding is a one-off on top: $449 self-service, $799 standard, $5,549 full. One small honesty check on their own page: the annual toggle promises “save up to 10%”, and the published annual figures work out at 8.3% on Core and 8.4% on Pro.

    How to actually use Unleashed Software

    Start with the 14-day trial, which runs with all features enabled, and use it to answer one question rather than to tour the product: how many sales orders does your busiest month actually produce? That number picks your tier and it is where the bill moves, because both bundles include only 100 a month and the upgrades run $70 to $490. Do the same for seats: Core covers 3 and Pro 5, and each extra one is $69 or $89 a month, so a six-person team on Core is $207 a month on top before anything else.

    Decide about support during the trial too: phone and live chat sit in a paid tier at $99 or $239 a month, and it is easier to price that in now than to discover it after go-live. Budget for onboarding as a separate one-off line of $449 to $5,549 depending on how much hand-holding you want. Unleashed is part of The Access Group.

    What happens if I go over 100 sales orders in a month?

    You are not billed per order; you are moved up a tier. Unleashed's own pricing FAQ says each plan has a free allocation and that if you exceed it in a given month, “an additional allocation of orders will be applied to your account in the months following”. Those allocations are the published ladder: $70 a month up to 500 orders, $200 up to 1,500, $320 up to 3,000 and $490 for unlimited. Since the cheapest step is $70, any business that routinely passes 100 orders a month should price Core at $469 rather than $399, and with a handful of extra seats and paid support on top, the real monthly figure moves well clear of the headline. Read on unleashedsoftware.com/pricing, 16 September and 4 October 2026.

    Does using Unleashed in the browser or through its managed integrations eat into the API allowance?

    No, per the Unleashed API FAQ (apidocs.unleashedsoftware.com/Faq, read 23 October 2026): browser use and the managed integrations it lists do not count as API usage. The 250,000 (Core) and 500,000 (Pro) call allowances are therefore only spent by your own or third-party API connections.

    Can the Unleashed API notify my app when a new order comes in?

    No push model is documented. The API FAQ (read 23 October 2026) says you need to poll the API to check for new data, a pull model. A custom integration that checks often spends calls from the bundle's API allowance, so size the polling interval against it.
