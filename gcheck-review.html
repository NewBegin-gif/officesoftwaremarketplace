The pricing page reads like self-serve and is not.

Best for

US employers running a steady number of hires, especially in healthcare, nonprofits, education and staffing, who want published per-check prices with no setup fee or contract and are happy to open an account through GCheck's sales team rather than a checkout.

Summarised from the pricing notes below; the notes carry the conditions.

Three packages, each priced per background check, with no setup fees, no minimums and no long-term contracts. Basic is $19.95 per check and covers an SSN trace and address history, a national criminal database search, the nationwide sex offender registry, the OIG excluded parties list, OFAC sanctions lists, global watchlists and a county criminal search in the candidate's current county of residence only.

Standard, marked Most Popular, is $39.95 per check and adds biometric identity verification and county criminal searches in every county on the candidate's seven-year address history. Advanced, marked Recommended, is $49.95 per check and adds a federal criminal search across every federal district on that same seven-year history. An asterisk on all three prices reads: additional third-party fees may apply to some products and are passed through with no markup; the page does not list those fees.

Full pricing notes (2 more paragraphs)

Employers running more than 50 checks a year are pointed to a custom quote, which is where the page places ATS and HRIS integrations and customised workflows, and GCheck states that volume pricing and merit-based nonprofit discounts exist without publishing either. Everything else in the catalogue — drug tests (DOT, 5-panel and 10-panel), motor vehicle and CDL records, employment, education and reference verification, credit, bankruptcy, eviction, liens and judgments searches, fingerprinting, Form I-9 and E-Verify, and international searches — carries no public price.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

“Pay per screen” sits above three published prices, but every Get Started with Basic, Standard or Advanced button on that page opens the same contact form as Book a Demo, asking for your company name. There is no checkout: an employer who wants to run one check this afternoon starts with a sales conversation, and the price you are quoted after it is the one to hold them to. The $19.95 check is narrower than the headline suggests.

Basic searches county court records only in the candidate's current county of residence, and GCheck's own FAQ calls the county search the gold standard for criminal record accuracy while describing the national database search as covering participating jurisdictions. A candidate who moved last year has their previous counties searched only from Standard at $39.95, which is the package GCheck itself marks Most Popular. Then there is the asterisk under every price: third-party fees may apply to some products and are passed through with no markup, and nothing on the site says which products or how much.

County court access fees are the usual example in this industry, so budget for the package price plus fees you will see for the first time on an invoice. The rest of the catalogue is unpriced. Drug tests, driving records, employment and education verification, credit and civil searches, fingerprinting and international searches are all listed and none carries a figure, so a role that needs an MVR or an employment verification on top of the criminal search is a quote, not a sum.

The same goes for integrations: GCheck's integrations page names 18 ATS and HRIS systems, including Greenhouse, Lever, Workday, BambooHR and Rippling, but the pricing page places integrations under the custom quote for employers running more than 50 checks a year, and its FAQ sends you to sales for the list. On track record we can tell you less than we would like. The 12–24 hour average turnaround and same-day results in many regions are GCheck's own figures.

Its Better Business Bureau profile and the usual review sites blocked our reader, and a Reddit search turned up no thread about GCheck specifically, so we found no independent account of turnaround or accuracy to weigh against the site's claims. GCheck does publish an FCRA dispute form for candidates with a stated 30-day response. We have not ordered a check from GCheck.

What GCheck does

US employment background screening sold per check: criminal, identity, sanctions and driving records, verifications, drug testing and I-9/E-Verify, with FCRA workflows and ATS and HRIS integrations for hiring teams.

Standard is the real floor

The three packages share the same base — SSN trace, national criminal database, sex offender registry, OIG, OFAC and global watchlists — and differ in how far the court searches reach. Basic at $19.95 searches county court records in one county, the one the candidate lives in now. Standard at $39.95 searches every county on their seven-year address history and adds biometric identity verification. For most hiring that is the step that matters, because county courts are where GCheck itself says the accurate criminal records sit, and a national database is a list of participating jurisdictions rather than every court.

Paying $20 more per hire for seven years of counties is cheap next to a missed record. Advanced at $49.95 adds federal district court searches, which is where fraud, embezzlement and other white-collar and interstate cases are filed. Worth the extra $10 for finance, healthcare billing and other roles that handle money or regulated data; unnecessary for most others. Whatever you choose, ask for the third-party fee schedule before the first order, and above 50 checks a year ask for the volume price and the ATS integration in the same conversation.

How to actually use GCheck

Decide the package before you call. List the roles you hire for and the searches each one legally needs: a driving job needs an MVR, a healthcare job usually calls for the OIG exclusion check, which every package carries, a finance role may justify the federal search in Advanced. Then ask GCheck for three things in writing: the third-party fees you should expect on a typical check in your states, the price of each add-on you need, and whether your ATS is connected at your volume or only above 50 checks a year.

Get the FCRA steps right on your side, because the screening company cannot do them for you. The candidate must receive a standalone disclosure and give written authorisation before the check runs, and if a report leads you to reject someone you owe them a pre-adverse action notice with a copy of the report and a summary of their rights, time to respond, and then a final adverse action notice.

GCheck lists adverse action administration among its compliance services and publishes a dispute form for candidates, which you can point them to. Time your first few checks against the 12–24 hour average GCheck advertises before you build it into an offer deadline.
