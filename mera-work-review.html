What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Mera Work keeps your records for 90 days. The pricing table says data retention up to three months, and the privacy policy spells it out: unless your order form says otherwise, customer data is held on a rolling 90-day basis and anything older may be deleted automatically from production. That rules it out as the place you look back from over a year: an annual review, a client disputing hours billed last spring, a payroll audit.

Export the timesheets and attendance you need every month, or get a longer retention period written into the order form before you roll it out. This is monitoring software, not only a timer. The feature table lists screenshots, live screen streaming, keyboard and mouse activity tracking and a stealth mode. Mera Work's own pages are more careful than the word keystroke suggests: its keystroke feature records how much typing happened and when, not the keys or words themselves, and screenshots can be blurred and limited by role.

But whether you may run screenshots, live view or a hidden agent on your staff's computers is a question for your employment adviser, not the vendor, and data-protection rules in Europe generally expect employees to be told what is recorded. Decide the policy first and tell people what the agent captures. Two practical points. The company behind it, Aapna Infotheek Private Limited, is based in New Delhi, and its privacy policy says data may be hosted or processed in India; if you need it kept elsewhere, ask about the private cloud configuration the feature table lists and put it in the contract.

And the review sites we normally read, Capterra and G2, blocked our reader on 30 September 2026. The one we could read, SoftwareSuggest, shows six reviews from 2025 under the old name Mera Monitor, and one of the three visible there also runs as a testimonial on Mera Work's own pricing page, so we found no clearly independent account of reliability or support to weigh. We have not run Mera Work ourselves.

What Mera Work does

Employee monitoring and time tracking for remote, hybrid and office teams: automatic timesheets, attendance and shifts, app and website usage, screenshots and live screen view, all in one per-user plan.

When the paid plan is worth it

Mera Work sells one plan, so the paid decision is about billing, not tiers. Every feature on the table, screenshots and live screen streaming included, costs $4 per user a month billed monthly or $3 billed annually (₹325 or ₹250 in rupees), read on 30 September 2026. For a 25-person team that is $1,200 a year on monthly billing against $900 on annual. The annual rate only saves money if your headcount holds: the cancellation and refund policy (last updated August 2026) says refunds are not automatic, SaaS cancellation generally takes effect at the end of the current billing term, and no refund is given for unused user licences during an active billing period.

Run the 14-day trial on your real team, then commit to annual for the seats you are sure of. If a renewal slips, the help desk FAQ describes a 7-day grace period before the account is suspended. Enterprise is a custom quote for 1,000 or more users, so most buyers never reach that step. What paying more does not fix is data retention. The plan's feature table caps it at three months, and the privacy policy describes a rolling 90-day window unless your order form says otherwise, so a longer history is a contract negotiation, not a billing choice.

Annual billing also does not settle whether you may run screenshots, live view or stealth mode on your staff; that stays your legal question.

How to actually use Mera Work

Setup is admin-first, and the order matters. Sign up through the Start for Free form, confirm the activation email and you land in the admin web app at app.mera.work. The help desk FAQ has you open Configuration > Setting > User Default Settings, enable the features you want and save before you press '+ Add Member', so decide there what the agent records and tell your staff before anyone is invited.

Add people one at a time (name, email and role) or by bulk upload from the sample file. New members sit in an inactive list until you activate them; they then get an activation email and set a password. Each person installs the desktop agent from the Download page (Windows 10 or later, macOS 10.11 or later, RHEL and Ubuntu-based Linux; Fedora and CentOS are untested) and logs in, and the timer starts automatically whenever the machine is active.

Three settings decide whether the reports mean anything: your organisation's time zone (daily email reports go out before 09:30 local time), the idle-time interval, and the list of apps and websites you class as productive, which can be set per organisation, department or user. The agent syncs every 10 minutes, so a fresh install looks empty at first.

Frequently asked questions

What does Mera Work cost per user?

$3 per user a month billed annually or $4 billed monthly, read on 30 September 2026; in rupees ₹250 or ₹325. That is one plan with every feature, and the pricing page says there are no add-ons. Enterprise is a custom quote for 1,000 or more users, and the sign-up page offers a 14-day trial without a card.

How long does Mera Work keep our data?

Three months by default. The feature table lists data retention up to three months, and the privacy policy describes a rolling 90-day window unless your order form says otherwise. If you need timesheets or attendance for a year-end review, payroll audit or client dispute, export them monthly or get a longer period written into the order form.

Can we cancel or drop seats partway through an annual plan?

Usually not with money back. Under Mera Work's own cancellation terms a SaaS subscription runs to the end of the term you paid for, a refund is something the company may consider rather than something you are owed, and seats you stop using mid-term are not refunded. Put on annual billing only the headcount you are sure of and keep the uncertain seats monthly.

What should I weigh before buying HR & People software?

Compliance is country by country, so the honest answer is: only where the vendor says it is, and only for the entity type you actually have. Ask which countries are covered by its own entities versus a local partner, who carries the liability if something is filed wrong, and how changes in local law reach you. Get those answers in the contract rather than the sales deck.
