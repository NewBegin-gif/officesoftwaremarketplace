What buyers report about Lindy.ai

Hands-on: tested 2026-10-31Reported by buyers: G2, Trustpilot, Reddit, Capterra and Product Hunt, read August 2026 (Capterra and Product Hunt read 12 August 2026)

Themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026 (Capterra and Product Hunt read 12 August 2026). We tested Lindy.ai ourselves on 2026-10-31, but one test is a narrow sample, so here is what other buyers report. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Setup is easy: that is the most common praise across 171 G2 reviews.
  • • For supervised work (inbox triage, meeting notes, scheduling) reviewers say it does what it claims.
  • • On Product Hunt, users single out that it carries tasks across their other tools rather than only producing text, with ready-made routines for calendars, appointment reminders and reworking existing content.
  • • A Capterra reviewer praises how far the templates bend to a specific workflow: in their case reshaping a clinical note template to their own house format.
What they complain about
  • • Cost is the single most-mentioned complaint (42 mentions), followed by subscription price (35). The cause reviewers named in August 2026 was the credit system: tasks then cost roughly $0.01 to $0.10+ each depending on model, so a predictable subscription became a variable bill. Lindy has since moved to per-user plans with a monthly credit level per seat, and in November 2026 added paid top-ups; see the price section for the current terms.
  • • Several reviewers report charges above their expected cap. Complex agent chains burn credits faster than people expect.
  • • Heavy users hit credit walls mid-cycle.
  • • Unsupervised reliability is where the complaints concentrate, and support is hard to reach on individual plans.
  • • A veterinary practice on Capterra reports that Lindy fails to follow clear, specific instructions more than half the time, that call recordings sometimes come back unusable, and that the AI both omits and invents points from a conversation. The same reviewer describes freezing, display problems, and support that answers by pointing at a tutorial video.
  • • Product Hunt users warn that the built-in AI for editing a workflow gets it wrong often enough that you should duplicate the workflow before letting it change anything.

Where the ratings disagree. 4.9 on G2 across 170+ reviews against 2.4 on Trustpilot. The G2 reviews are about whether it works; the Trustpilot ones are about billing, and both are true. Capterra and Product Hunt, read in August 2026, do not open a new gap so much as sharpen the existing one: the praise is still about supervised, templated work and the complaints are still about unattended reliability and support: now with a specific clinical-transcription example behind them rather than a general impression.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is no tier ladder any more, only a credit dial on one Team plan, so the questions are how many credits to buy and who takes a seat. Read on lindy.ai/pricing on 8 November 2026, the dial starts at either $29.99 for 3,000 credits or $49.99 for 5,000: the page served both versions that day, and our own sign-up on 31 October asked for $49.99 a month after the trial.

Budget on $49.99 until you see your own checkout. Bigger levels are cheaper per credit, up to a point. The entry levels and the $10-per-1,000 top-up all work out at one cent a credit. 15,000 credits for $99.99 is about 0.67 cents and 35,000 for $199.99 about 0.57 cents. The next step, 45,000 for $299.99, is back at about 0.67 cents, more per credit than the level below it. The team levels from 70,000 credits are exact multiples of the $199.99 level and keep its rate.

Seats are the meter most teams underestimate. Anyone who uses Lindy takes a seat, including a colleague who only @mentions it in Slack. Those who join through Slack get a 7-day trial first; direct signups are billed immediately. A seat you remove stays active to the end of the cycle, with no mid-cycle proration. The page also says every active user is billed from the entry amount; how that combines with a team credit level it does not spell out, so ask before a team rollout.

What upgrading does not fix: monthly credits still expire at the end of each cycle, and the work sizes are still ranges (2-250 credits for an everyday ask, 1,000-2,500 for a big build), so the first month is a measurement rather than a plan. The new top-up does remove the old hard stop: when the pool runs dry Lindy pauses credit-using actions, and an admin can now buy more at $10 per 1,000 instead of waiting for the reset.

    How to actually use Lindy.ai

    Decide who can @mention Lindy in Slack before you roll it out, because seats drive the cost as much as usage does. Anyone who uses it takes a seat, including a colleague who only ever mentions it in a channel, and although people who join that way get a seven-day free trial first, direct signups are billed straight away. A seat you remove runs to the end of the cycle with no mid-cycle proration, so an accidental rollout is expensive to reverse.

    Use the free start as your measurement. The pricing page says it is $50 in credits for 7 days with no credit card required; our email sign-up on 31 October hit a card screen before the trial anyway. Run a week of real work and read how many credits it took: Lindy sizes an everyday ask at 2 to 250 credits, deep work at 250 to 1,000 and a big build at 1,000 to 2,500, so two big builds can use a whole 5,000-credit entry level on their own.

    Then set the dial from that week, not from headcount. Every seat's credits pool into one workspace balance that refreshes each cycle and does not roll over. If you run short, an admin can top up at $10 per 1,000 credits, and top-up credits stay until used; otherwise Lindy pauses credit-using actions until the reset, which matters for anything running in production. If you expect real volume, the 15,000 and 35,000 levels are the cheaper ones per credit, about 0.67 and 0.57 cents against one cent at entry and on top-ups. Skip 45,000 at $299.99, which is back at about 0.67.

    Did Lindy's price change?

    Twice in 2026. We capture their pricing page monthly: on 7 July and again on 2 August 2026 the three published prices were $49, $99 and $199. On 12 August 2026 the same page read $29.99, $99.99 and $199.99 per user per month. By 8 November 2026 the three tiers were gone: the page sells one Team plan where you pick a monthly credit level, and the entry level differed between fetches that day, $29.99 for 3,000 credits or $49.99 for 5,000. The 15,000- and 35,000-credit levels kept the old Pro and Max amounts, $99.99 and $199.99. If you were quoted one entry price, check which one your checkout shows.

    Who counts as a paid seat?

    Anyone who uses Lindy, and the definition is wider than most teams expect. Their own pricing FAQ (read 12 August 2026) says a seat is taken whether someone signs up directly, joins your workspace, or only @mentions Lindy in Slack. Colleagues who arrive that way get a 7-day free trial first; direct signups are billed immediately. Removing a seat does not refund anything: it stays active to the end of the cycle you paid for, with no mid-cycle proration.

    What happens when the credits run out?

    Lindy pauses credit-using actions until your credits reset at the start of the next cycle, and there is no surprise bill. What changed is the way out: read on lindy.ai/pricing on 8 November 2026, admins can now top up at $10 per 1,000 credits or move to more monthly credits at any time, where until October the page offered no top-up. Monthly credits do not roll over, but top-up credits stay until you use them. Plan allowances are described in ranges (an everyday ask is 2-250 credits, a big build 1,000-2,500), so how far your pool goes is an estimate. Reviewers on Capterra and Product Hunt in August 2026 report burning through more than they planned on complex chains.

    If someone leaves the team, can we re-use their free week?

    No. Read on lindy.ai/pricing on 12 September and again on 2 October 2026: the free week is one-time per teammate, so anyone who leaves and comes back goes straight onto a billed seat with immediate access. Their FAQ also says Lindy will not re-add anyone an admin removed, and that a removed seat stays active to the end of the cycle with no mid-cycle proration. The one lever in the other direction is new on the same page: admins can set credit allocations per seat, so a shared pool no longer has to be first-come, first-served.

    Will Lindy act without my approval?

    Not according to its pricing FAQ, read 8 November 2026: anything with outside impact, such as sending an email, updating a ticket, posting to another channel or publishing a doc, waits for approval, while read-only lookups from approved sources do not need it. We could not check this ourselves, because our trial on 31 October stopped at the card screen.
