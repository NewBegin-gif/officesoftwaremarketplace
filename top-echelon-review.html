Top Echelon Review (2026)

HR & PeopleResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-03

Almost everything people shop for sits in Professional, not Standard: Smart-TE AI, automations, sequences, inbound email sync and API access are all one tier up, so Standard is a competent but plain ATS.

Best for: recruiting agencies and SMB staffing firms that want an applicant tracking system and CRM in one place, especially ones that would actually work split placements with other recruiters.
Who should skip it: if you are an in-house talent team hiring for your own company — this is built around agency and split-placement workflows, so you would carry a network you never use.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 25 Aug 2026

TE Recruit (the ATS and CRM) runs $75 per seat per month billed annually or $89 month-to-month on Standard, and $89 billed annually or $110 month-to-month on Professional. TE Network, the split-placement network, is priced per agency rather than per seat: $135 per month billed annually with the $350 startup fee waived, or $150 per month billed monthly plus a $350 one-time startup fee (2026). Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The bigger thing to price honestly is that TE Recruit and TE Network are two separate subscriptions. The network is billed per agency on top of your seats, so "Top Echelon" can mean two bills. And taking the network month-to-month adds a $350 one-time startup fee that annual billing waives.

What Top Echelon does

Recruiting software for agencies and staffing firms: TE Recruit is an ATS and CRM with resume parsing, pipeline management, hotlists and job-board integrations, while TE Network is a split-placement network for sharing roles and candidates with other recruiting firms.

What buyers report about Top Echelon

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Top Echelon ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Support is the standout — reviewers repeatedly call it responsive and willing to go further than expected. Sentiment rating of 92 across 1,575 reviews.
  • • Value for a small agency: from around $75 per user per month on annual billing, with low start-up cost and flexible user management.
What they complain about
  • • Email integration is the consistent weak point. Reviewers report trouble linking Outlook and Gmail, conversations not tracking, and attachments failing.
  • • The interface is described as clunky by a meaningful number of reviewers.
  • • Costs climb once you bundle services or add users, so price it at the headcount you expect rather than today's.

When the paid plan is worth it

Read this as two products with two pricing models; buying the wrong one is easy. TE Recruit is the ATS and CRM and is priced per seat: Standard at $75 a seat a month billed annually or $89 month-to-month, Professional at $89 annually or $110 monthly. TE Network is the split-placement network and is priced per agency rather than per seat, at $135 a month billed annually or $150 monthly.

On TE Network the annual commitment is worth more than the headline suggests. Paying monthly costs $15 more a month, which is $180 across a year -- but it also carries a one-time $350 start-up fee that the annual plan waives. First-year difference: about $530, not $180. From year two the gap narrows to the $180. On TE Recruit the step from Standard to Professional is $14 a seat a month on the annual rate, and the pricing page does spell out what it buys: Smart-TE AI, automations, sequences, inbound email sync and API access, plus ten times the storage and weekly rather than monthly backups.

And a seat-priced product and an agency-priced product behave very differently as you hire: adding recruiters multiplies TE Recruit and leaves TE Network untouched.

How to actually use Top Echelon

Top Echelon's own guide puts navigation first, then records, then email — and email carries the one hard prerequisite. If you are not integrating with Microsoft or Google, you must "Verify Your Domain for TE Recruit to send email on your behalf," so handle that before any outreach. With the Google integration on, sends of ten recipients or fewer leave from your Gmail account while mass emails still go from Top Echelon's servers.

Records get in four ways: typed by hand, emailed in as resumes through the parser, imported as spreadsheets through the Contact Importer, or captured with the browser extension.

Distilled from Top Echelon Help Center: Getting Started with TE Recruit, Converting Your Database From Another Software, and Google Integration (help.topechelon.com), read 20 August 2026.

Common Top Echelon setup mistakes

Where Top Echelon setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

The step people underestimate is bringing an existing database across.

  • It is a paid, scheduled service — $500 on an annual plan, $1,000 month-to-month — run by a data conversion specialist through a mapping session and a DropSend transfer, typically one to two weeks, first-come.
  • Then the 30-day window bites: "After 30 days, we will no longer hold your data backup from your previous ATS," and later fixes run $100 an hour against a backup you supply.

The natural comparison is Bullhorn or Recruit CRM — the agency-ATS incumbents.

What should I weigh before buying HR & People software?

Compliance is country by country, so the honest answer is: only where the vendor says it is, and only for the entity type you actually have. Ask which countries are covered by its own entities versus a local partner, who carries the liability if something is filed wrong, and how changes in local law reach you. Get those answers in the contract rather than the sales deck.
