Bills, travel and receipts: three tools that each meter you differently

Researched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-01

None of these three charges you per seat, and that is the most important thing about them.

Money leaves a small company through three doors: supplier invoices, trips, and the pile of receipts that arrives afterwards. The tools here each stand at one of those doors. They overlap less than their marketing suggests, and the interesting comparison is not feature lists but where each one starts charging.

All three break the usual per-seat pattern, which is good news for a growing team and bad news for anyone trying to forecast the bill from a pricing page. A free tier that meters transactions can be cheaper than a paid tier for years, and then stop being cheaper in a single busy month.

How we picked

  • Figures come from the vendor’s own page, with the date we read them. Where a vendor publishes nothing, we say that rather than guessing.
  • We name the unit being metered, because with these three the unit is the whole story.
  • We say who should skip each one — in this category the most common mistake is buying a second tool that duplicates something your accounting software already does.

The picks

1M
Melio
Best for: small businesses that want to pay bills by bank transfer or card and get paid, starting free with no subscription

Starts genuinely free and charges by transaction rather than by month, which makes it the low-commitment way to move accounts payable out of a bank portal. The thing to check before adopting it is whether your accounting software already does bill-pay: if it does, a second tool mostly adds another place to look.

Skip it if you already run AP through robust accounting software with bill-pay built in — a second tool adds little

Best for: companies that want self-serve travel booking with built-in policy control — genuinely free — and optionally unified exp

The travel booking itself carries no fee, which is unusual enough to be worth stating plainly. Navan publishes no price list for the wider product, and we could not retrieve one in either July or August 2026, so anything beyond the free booking layer is a conversation rather than a number.

A free tier is a meter, not a gift

Two of the three tools here start at nothing, and in both cases the free plan is a metered plan with the meter set generously. That is a perfectly fair arrangement, but it means the question to ask is not ‘what does it cost’ but ‘what happens at volume’ — and the answer should come from your own transaction count, not from the tier the vendor marks Popular.

The second thing worth checking is where the data lands. A capture tool that does not push cleanly into the ledger you already keep has moved the work rather than removed it.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a bill-pay tool if my accounting software has one?

Usually not. The case for a separate tool is approval workflow, paying suppliers who want a method your ledger does not support, or getting payments out of a single person’s bank login — not the payment itself.

Why is travel booking free?

Because the booking is not where the money is. Travel platforms earn on the supplier side and on the expense and card products they attach, which is why the free layer can be genuinely free and the rest is quote-only.

What should I count before buying a receipt tool?

Documents per month, not users. These tools price on volume, so a five-person firm processing three hundred invoices pays more than a twenty-person firm processing forty.
