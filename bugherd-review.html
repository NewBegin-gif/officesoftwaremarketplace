BugHerd Review (2026)

By Daan Haket · Tested hands-on · written with an AI assistant · who does what

The per-team-member tiers are the thing to size honestly: your clients report for free as guests, but everyone on your side who works the feedback needs a seat, so a growing agency steps up tiers.

Best for

web agencies and site teams that want clients to point-and-click feedback directly on the page — and, new in 2026, want AI agents (Claude Code, Cursor and others) to read, triage and action that feedback via BugHerd's MCP server, free in beta.

Skip it if

you don't work with external clients or website revisions (a plain issue tracker is enough), or you need deep dev-workflow tracking that belongs in Jira.

When the paid plan is worth it

BugHerd's price list runs Standard, Studio, Premium and a Custom tier quoted as "Let's talk". Standard is $50/mo month-to-month or $42/mo billed yearly and covers 5 members with 10GB storage; Studio is $80 or $67 for 10 members and 25GB; Premium is $150 or $125 for 25 members and 50GB. Every tier already includes unlimited projects and unlimited client users, so moving up buys seats, storage and a short list of gated features rather than scale.

Standard to Studio adds only members and storage. The genuine jump is Studio to Premium, which unlocks custom branding, letting guests see other guests' and members' feedback, the Guest Task Board, and the Jira, Asana, Linear, ClickUp and Monday.com integrations; SSO and a dedicated success manager sit only in Custom. Extra members cost $8 per user monthly or $6.60 on annual billing, so teams slightly over their seat count should price add-on seats against the next tier.

Note that the pricing table places auto-tagging and similar task detection under Premium, while BugHerd's AI page says every AI feature is available to all users, most in free beta — their claim, not our measurement. Upgrading fixes none of the install and extension frictions below.

How to actually use BugHerd

BugHerd's Getting Started guide is eight articles: an overview plus seven chapters. Chapter 1 has you create a project from "My projects", give it a name and an initial website URL, then add further URLs under "Websites & Files"; a project can span multiple domains or sub-sections. Chapter 2 covers installation, where the route recommended for non-technical users is pasting BugHerd's JavaScript snippet into the site's head section, with Google Tag Manager and a WordPress plugin as alternatives.

The extension route needs no code, but the docs concede members and clients also need the browser extension, calling it "a hurdle to smooth adoption". Invites go out one at a time — "you can only invite a single user at a time to the Team" — and each invitee must create a BugHerd account. The real friction is the sidebar failing to appear. BugHerd's troubleshooting article blames content security policies that block the script, ad blockers and uBlock Origin's EasyPrivacy filter, blocked third-party cookies, WordPress optimisation plugins minifying the script, self-hosting the script (unsupported), and the same domain being used across different projects.

Frequently asked questions

Does BugHerd's trial require a credit card?

Sign-up check 2026-10-24: card not requested in this account; trial length confirmed: 7 days.

Who counts as a paid member, and who can join for free?

Your own team counts; clients do not. The pricing page (bugherd.com/pricing, read 13 October 2026) limits Members — 'Devs, designers, PMs, QAs' — to 5 on Standard, 10 on Studio and 25 on Premium, with extra members at $8 a month or $6.60 billed annually, while Clients/stakeholders are unlimited on every plan. BugHerd's roles article (read the same day) says clients can create tasks, comment and see the tasks they created, but cannot see the project board below Premium. Owners, Managers and Billing Users get Member access plus project, user or billing rights; whether those roles each take a paid seat is not confirmed.

Can my clients see each other's feedback?

Not on Standard or Studio. The pricing comparison (read 13 October 2026) marks 'Guests see other guest feedback', 'Let guest users see member feedback', member-only commenting, the Guest Task Board and scheduling a feedback deadline as Premium and Custom only. On the two lower plans each client sees the tasks they reported.

Can I take my data with me, and can I get my money back?

Yes to both, per the pricing-page FAQ (read 13 October 2026): every plan supports data export as CSV, XML and JSON, plus access through the BugHerd API, and there is a 60-day money-back guarantee — 'If you're not satisfied within 60 days of purchase, contact us and we'll refund your subscription.' What happens when you downgrade with more members than the smaller plan allows is not confirmed.
