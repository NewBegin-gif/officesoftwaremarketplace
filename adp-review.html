Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 29 Aug 2026

(2026-08-15, read on their own site) ADP shows no public pricing for RUN anywhere on its site, and we checked four places before saying so. /pricing returns a 404, and so does /pricing.md. The RUN product page offers only a “Get Pricing” button. And the dedicated package-comparison page lists all four tiers with “Get pricing” under each one and not a single figure.

The tiers are Essential Payroll, Enhanced Payroll (adds ZipRecruiter, State Unemployment Insurance and garnishment payments), Complete Payroll & HR Plus (adds basic HR support) and HR Pro Payroll & HR (enhanced HR support and staff perks). A promotion was running when we read it: three months of free payroll, marked “Ends 8/27”, with terms behind an asterisk. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

You cannot compare ADP against anyone without first giving a salesperson your headcount and your phone number. And the three-months-free banner carries an end date, which puts a clock on a decision you cannot yet cost. Ask for the per-employee-per-month rate and the per-payroll-run fee separately. Ask what happens in month four. And ask what the price becomes at double your current headcount, because payroll pricing steps rather than scales smoothly.

One detail worth noticing: ADP does publish an /llms.txt, a file whose entire purpose is to guide AI assistants around its site. They are willing to machine-readably explain what they sell. They are not willing to say what it costs.

What ADP does

Payroll, tax filing and HR for small and mid-sized employers, sold as four RUN packages: Essential, Enhanced, Complete and HR Pro. No public pricing anywhere — /pricing is a 404 and every tier on the comparison page says “Get pricing”, so budgeting means a sales call.

What buyers report about ADP

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run ADP ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Small employers on Capterra consistently describe payroll runs and tax filings as quick and dependable, with payroll, employee records and basic HR in one place. Several say they switched from QuickBooks Online or chose it over Gusto for that.
  • • Buyers rate support highly there too (4.5 for customer service across 1,523 reviews), which is the exact opposite of the picture on Trustpilot.
What they complain about
  • • Reporting is the most repeated buyer complaint: fine for standard payroll summaries, rigid the moment you want a custom report or want to cross-reference data.
  • • Time tracking, benefits management and advanced HR sit behind extra subscriptions, so the quoted number is not the running cost.
  • • Multi-jurisdiction payroll: a construction controller (February 2026) reports RUN could not handle employees living in one state and working across job sites in others.

Where the ratings disagree. The two platforms disagree completely: 4.6 across 1,523 reviews on Capterra against 1.2 across 3,081 on Trustpilot. The reason is who writes where. Capterra's reviewers are the employers who bought the payroll service; Trustpilot's are overwhelmingly employees of ADP's clients, writing about the app they clock in on, logins that loop through identity re-confirmation, and deposits landing late. Note also that the Trustpilot distribution is not what a 1.2 suggests: the platform shows 39% five-star against 48% one-star, so it is a split record compressed by TrustScore's weighting rather than a uniformly bad one. Read Capterra to choose a payroll provider; read Trustpilot to know what your staff will experience.

What actually drives the quote

There is nothing to compare, because ADP publishes no price for RUN anywhere. We checked four separate places again on 29 August 2026 before saying so. /pricing returns a 404, and so does /pricing.md. The RUN product page offers only a Get Pricing button. And the dedicated package-comparison page lists all four tiers — Essential Payroll, Enhanced Payroll, Complete Payroll & HR Plus and HR Pro Payroll & HR — with Get pricing under each one and not a single figure.

Quote-only is normal in payroll, so the complaint is not that ADP hides its price. It is that you cannot compare ADP against anyone without first handing a salesperson your headcount and your phone number. That makes the preparation more important than usual. Payroll pricing typically has two components that vendors prefer to quote as one: a per-employee-per-month rate and a per-payroll-run fee. Ask for those separately, because a business paying weekly runs four or five times as many run fees as one paying monthly, and a single blended number hides that entirely.

Then ask what the price becomes at double your current headcount. Payroll pricing usually steps at thresholds rather than scaling smoothly, and the step you are about to cross is the one that matters. There was a promotion running when we read the site: three months of free payroll, with terms behind an asterisk. Its end date had moved — 8/27 when we read the page on 15 August 2026, 9/24 when we read it again on 29 August 2026.

Note what that does to a quote-only product. It puts a deadline on a decision you cannot yet cost, which is precisely the wrong order to make a payroll decision in — and a deadline that moves is not really one. The question to ask is not what the discount is worth, but what happens in month four. That is the number you will be paying for years, and it is the one the banner does not mention.

Finally, price the right product. If you only pay 1099 contractors, ADP sells that separately from the packages above, so quoting the packages will overstate your cost. And if you are a one or two person company, a published-price competitor covers the same job without the sales call. The inability to budget before talking to someone is itself a cost, and for a small employer it is often the deciding one.

How to actually use ADP

Write down four numbers before you take the call. You cannot get a price without giving a salesperson your details, so arrive prepared: headcount, pay frequency, the states you operate in, and whether you need HR support or only payroll. Those four determine which of Essential, Enhanced, Complete or HR Pro you will be quoted. Ask for the price in two parts, because payroll pricing has two parts: the per-employee-per-month rate and the per-payroll-run fee.

A quote given as one monthly figure hides which of those is driving it, and they behave very differently if you move from monthly to fortnightly pay runs. Then ask the two questions that the promotion makes urgent. What happens in month four, when the three-months-free offer ends — get the ongoing rate in writing, not the promotional one. And what the price becomes at double your current headcount. Payroll pricing usually steps rather than scaling smoothly, and the step is easier to negotiate before you sign than after you grow into it.

Do not let the offer's end date set your timetable. A banner with a deadline puts a clock on a decision you cannot yet cost, and any payroll provider will still want your business next month. Get competing quotes on the same two-part basis and compare them properly.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying HR & People software?

Compliance is country by country, so the honest answer is: only where the vendor says it is, and only for the entity type you actually have. Ask which countries are covered by its own entities versus a local partner, who carries the liability if something is filed wrong, and how changes in local law reach you. Get those answers in the contract rather than the sales deck.
