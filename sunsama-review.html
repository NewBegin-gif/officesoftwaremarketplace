We tested it · 2026-11-18

We created the account and our AI assistant ran the test in our browser. On 18 November 2026 the session set up a trial of Sunsama with every calendar and tool connection skipped. Ten tasks were typed by hand and the app filled in the time estimates itself. A prompt to the planning agent (focus on three tasks, about four hours of work) gave a day plan: the agent deferred 6 tasks and adjusted 3 tasks and the day showed Work: 4:05. From the first task at 19:00:09 to the first day plan at about 19:01:50 took about 1 minute and 45 seconds, of which the planning itself was about 25 seconds. The plan started at 19:00, after the end of the work day of 17:00 set in the setup. Pulling in tasks from other tools was not tested. The trial lasts 14 days without a card, there is no free plan after it, and Pro costs $17 a month billed yearly ($204) or $22 a month billed monthly. The Continue button was not clicked, so no checkout was seen, and cancelling was not tried.

What buyers report about Sunsama

Hands-on: tested 2026-11-18Reported by buyers: Product Hunt, Software Advice and practitioner reviews, read August 2026

Themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We tested Sunsama ourselves on 2026-11-18, but one test is a narrow sample, so here is what other buyers report. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • The planning and shutdown rituals are the product, and reviewers say they change how a day goes: scattered work becomes a realistic plan.
  • • Integrations with Jira, Asana, Todoist, Outlook and Google Calendar pull everything into one morning view.
What they complain about
  • • In August 2026 reviewers quoted $25 a month, or $20 on annual, as Sunsama's own FAQ then did (the pricing page has changed since; see the price section), and there is no free tier at all: Sunsama says deliberately so. Price is the most-named drawback.
  • • The mobile app cannot do advanced planning or the daily review, so the ritual only works at a desk.
  • • There is no AI layer at all in 2026 while every competitor has added scheduling and prioritisation.
  • • Project tracking is limited and the interface is starting to look dated.

One page, two prices

Pro is the only self-serve plan: $22 a month per person billed monthly, or $17 a month billed yearly ($204), with unlimited usage and integrations. Enterprise is quote-only (re-read 19 November 2026 on their own pricing page and llms.txt). Earlier this autumn the page contradicted itself: on 30 August and again on 19 September its FAQ said $25 a month, or $20 yearly, while the plan card said $22 and $17.

By 19 November the FAQ matched the plan card, so the number on the card is now the one to budget on. The annual discount is slightly better than the page claims. The manifesto still promises '20% off' for paying a year up front; $17 against $22 is about 23%, or $60 a year per person. The same manifesto still explains why they charge $25.00 and not $24.99. That line no longer matches any price on the page and does not change your bill.

The structural point is simpler: this is per person, with no team discount mentioned anywhere. Two people is two subscriptions, and the FAQ's own example is $44 a month for two ($34 billed annually). For a tool whose premise is daily planning and calendar integration, that is worth modelling across the whole team before anyone else asks for it. What paying does not fix: there is no free plan after the trial and no cheaper tier, so the only choice is whether to pay at all, and Enterprise adds governance (SSO and SAML, SCIM, audit logs) rather than a discount.

The free trial needs no card, which is the right way in. What you are testing is not features but whether the daily ritual (planning each day deliberately, in a tool, every morning) is one you keep doing after two weeks. That is a question about you rather than about the software, and no tier changes the answer.

    How to actually use Sunsama

    Budget on the plan card: $22 a month per person billed monthly, or $17 a month billed yearly ($204). Until September the FAQ on the same page said $25 and $20; on 19 November 2026 it matched the plan card, but check the number on the payment screen against the one that persuaded you anyway. Then start the free trial, no card is needed, and treat it as a test of a habit rather than of a feature list.

    The premise here is that you plan each day deliberately, every morning. What you need to know after two weeks is whether you still did it in week two, because that is the entire value and no tier affects it. Connect your real calendars and task sources on day one rather than trying it clean. Sunsama's argument is that it pulls everything into one daily plan, and that argument only becomes testable when everything is actually in there: including the sources that make your day messy.

    If you are buying for a team, multiply properly: this is per person with no team discount mentioned, and the FAQ's own example is $44 a month for two people ($34 billed annually).

No volume break is mentioned at any headcount. Enterprise exists but is contact-only and buys governance rather than a discount (SSO and SAML, SCIM and audit logs, custom privacy and data requirements) with no price published. For a daily-planning tool that only works if the whole team adopts the ritual, model the full headcount before anyone champions it.

    Is it worth waiting for a free tier or a Black Friday deal?

    No, and unusually they commit to that in writing. The pricing manifesto on this page says there is no free-forever plan and none is planned, no lifetime deals, and no fake sales (naming Black Friday and Cyber Monday specifically) on the grounds that the product is worth the same all year. It also explains they price in whole dollars rather than $24.99 because they consider that psychological trickery. The only concessions on offer are the annual commitment and the 14-day trial, which needs no card and is followed by a cancel button rather than a support queue.

    If I plan my Notion tasks in Sunsama, does ticking them off update Notion?

    According to Sunsama's Notion integration page (sunsama.com/integrations/notion, read 4 November 2026), Sunsama automatically updates your tasks in Notion when you import them or check them off. That is the vendor's description of two events, importing and completing; whether every other Notion field (dates, properties, edits made inside Sunsama) syncs back is not confirmed on that page, which points to Sunsama's integration docs for the detail. The page does not say the integration is limited to a particular plan.
