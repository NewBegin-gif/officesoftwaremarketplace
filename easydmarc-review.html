We tested it · 2026-11-15

We created the account and our AI assistant ran the test in our browser. On 15 November 2026 the session tested EasyDMARC on the platform trial: adding our own domain, the first result, the verification route, the price of the plans and the checkout up to the card form. No DNS was changed and nothing was paid. Add Domain first offers Discovered Domains, which asks to connect providers such as Mailgun, Amazon SES, SendGrid, HubSpot, Klaviyo and HighLevel; we chose Add Manually. Our domain was added as type Sending (the other choice is Parked) at about 17:28. The step Verify Domain Manually followed: EasyDMARC recognised Hostinger as the DNS provider and says to delete the current DMARC record of type TXT at host _dmarc and then to add a new CNAME record with a value under easydmarc.pro. So verifying means removing your own DMARC record and letting EasyDMARC host DMARC through a CNAME (managed DMARC); stopping later means putting your own record back, which is derived from the steps and not tested. We chose Later: a DNS change needs a separate approval from the account owner, so no DNS was changed and the domain stays unverified. The domain list showed Policy None, Total Volume 0 and a warning triangle. A domain overview was available within a minute and was viewed at 17:29: Infrastructure Health Score, Total Score 32, with Domain Health 80/100, Authentication 40/100, Sender Insights 0/100 and Threat Intelligence 0/100. Authentication showed DMARC Policy None, DMARC Record Valid, SPF Valid (3/10 lookups) and DKIM No Record with Selectors N/A; DMARC Compliance Rate was 0%. Blocklist was Inactive with Monitoring off and Google Postmaster had No data. Our own dig check at 17:29 showed that the _dmarc TXT record is v=DMARC1; p=none, so the policy None is right, and that the SPF record includes the Hostinger mail servers, which is right. The DKIM selector hostingermail-a is a CNAME to the DKIM host of the mail provider with a valid RSA key. So our domain does have DKIM, and EasyDMARC reported No Record, presumably because it does not know the selector until reports arrive, which only happens after the CNAME verification. Pending Actions showed 1 Critical, Verify your domain to unlock full visibility; without verification no DMARC reports arrive. No kit records were entered.

When the paid plan is worth it

Read the comparison table rather than the feature bullets, because what you are buying is domains, history and message volume, and since the page was rebuilt (read 4 October 2026) not seats: every tier, Plus included, now lists unlimited users. Plus at €44.99 a month (€35.99 billed annually) carries 2 domains, 3 months of history and a volume selector from 100,000 to 1,000,000 messages a month. Premium at €89.99 (€71.99 annually) carries 4 domains, 1 year of history and up to 5,000,000 messages, plus EasySPF, proactive DMARC alerts and AI agents that read the reports, with a dedicated customer-success manager on yearly billing only.

Above that, the old single Enterprise tier is now two quote-only plans: Deliverability adds DNS management, sender insights, inbox-placement tests and up to 3 years of history, and Enterprise: Email Trust adds threat intelligence plus SSO, API access and audit logs. If single sign-on is a procurement requirement, neither Plus nor Premium meets it. What no upgrade does is shorten the DNS work: SPF, DKIM and DMARC records still have to be published and moved to enforcement by you or your provider, and a higher tier only buys more history and volume to watch that happen.

    How to actually use EasyDMARC

    The unpaid route is useful first. Their free record checkers and generators (DMARC, SPF, DKIM, BIMI, MX and a domain scanner) let you see what your domain publishes today and generate a syntactically valid DMARC record before you sign up for anything. From there the pattern is the standard one: publish a DMARC TXT record at p=none with the reporting address pointing at the platform, let aggregate reports arrive, identify every legitimate sender they reveal, and only then tighten the policy.

    Two managed options exist to keep you out of DNS afterwards. Managed DMARC routes the record through a single CNAME so policy changes happen in the dashboard rather than at your registrar, and EasySPF flattens SPF dynamically to survive the ten-lookup limit after a one-time DNS change. The pricing page no longer lists a free plan, but Plus and Premium both open with a free trial and no card. With three months of history on Plus, start collecting reports well before you need to look back at them.

    What sets my EasyDMARC tier: domains or email volume?

    Both, and the volume selector is the one buyers miss. Read on easydmarc.com/pricing on 4 October 2026: the headline rate on each card is the floor for its smallest volume bracket, and each card carries its own selector on top. Plus runs from 100,000 through 500,000 to 1,000,000 messages a month; Premium adds a 5,000,000 step; the two quoted plans above it take custom volume. So 44.99 a month on Plus (35.99 billed annually) buys 2 domains and three months of report history at the smallest bracket, and 89.99 on Premium (71.99 annually) buys 4 domains, a year of history, EasySPF, proactive alerts and AI agents that read the reports. Users are unlimited on both, where on 17 September Plus allowed one invited user, so team size no longer pushes you up a tier. Annual billing is marked as 20% off plus bonus features.

    What happens if I go over my monthly email volume?

    You lose data rather than money. Read on 4 October 2026, the quota tooltip on the pricing page says that once you pass your monthly quota, additional reports are not processed until the next billing cycle. There is no overage rate on the page, so nothing extra is billed; the reports for the rest of that month simply never reach the dashboard. On 17 September the FAQ described it more harshly, as losing access to reports and dashboard until you upgrade. That FAQ is no longer on the page, so the tooltip is the current statement. Either way the risk lands in the situation you bought the tool for: a spoofing campaign that spikes your report volume is exactly the month the counter runs out. Size the plan on your busiest month.

    I have more than two domains: what does that cost?

    The page still will not give you a per-domain price. Read 4 October 2026: Plus covers 2 domains, Premium 4, and the two quoted plans, Deliverability and Enterprise: Email Trust, go up to unlimited. There is no published per-domain add-on rate, so a third domain means Premium and a fifth means a sales conversation. One rule works in your favour: subdomains are detected automatically and do not count toward the limit, so a marketing subdomain is not a second domain. Count your separately registered domains instead, including the parked and legacy ones, because those are exactly the domains an attacker will spoof and the reason the reporting exists. Data history scales with the same ladder: 3 months on Plus, 1 year on Premium, up to 3 years on the quoted plans.

    Do subdomains count against my domain limit?

    Only if you add them yourself. The pricing page says subdomains are detected automatically and do not count, but EasyDMARC's help article on managed solutions for subdomains, read 21 October 2026, adds the catch: to run Managed DMARC, EasySPF or Managed DKIM on a subdomain you must add it to the platform as a separate domain, and it then counts against your account limit. With Plus at 2 domains and Premium at 4, a root domain plus one separately managed sending subdomain already fills Plus. If reporting is all you need, leave subdomains under the root: they inherit its DMARC policy, show up in its reports, and the sp= tag sets a separate subdomain policy.

    What do I have to undo if I leave EasyDMARC?

    That depends on whether you used the managed services. The help article How to Offboard a Domain, read 21 October 2026: with Managed DMARC, EasySPF or Managed DKIM active, deactivate them first, copy every DKIM record EasyDMARC holds into your own DNS zone, check none is missing, and only then delete the Managed DKIM NS record at the _domainkey prefix; doing it in another order risks breaking authentication. Without managed services you only replace the EasyDMARC addresses in your DMARC record's rua and ruf tags. The managed features are the convenience you pay for and also the part that takes work to leave. Exporting report history is not covered in that article and is not confirmed.
