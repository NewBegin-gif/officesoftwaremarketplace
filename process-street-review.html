We tested it · 2026-10-23

Tested by our team on 23 October 2026 during the Pro trial, without a card and without integrations: Daan created the account and our AI assistant ran the test in Daan's browser. We had the AI build a purchase-approval workflow (purchase ID and amount, a budget and a supplier check, manager approval, at most six steps), ran it twice with known amounts, completed one run with approval, left the other open, and exported the Reports views.

How we tested

  1. Completed the workflow trial onboarding
  2. Generated a purchase-approval workflow from instructions
  3. Started two runs and entered purchase IDs and amounts
  4. Completed and approved one run and checked Reports
  5. Downloaded active and completed run views with Export all columns
  • Generate with AI (a name plus instructions) produced the workflow in about 30 seconds: exactly the six steps we asked for, with full task texts and checklists, 35 form fields, 6 dynamic due dates, 1 approval step and 0 stop tasks. Starting a run added a seventh task, 'Role assignments', with four roles the AI invented, such as procurement analyst and finance analyst. That is a lot of fields for a simple approval.
  • Tasks could be ticked off while most of the 35 fields were still empty: no field was required. Values we entered were saved straight away, and completing a task gave the next one a due date automatically.
  • The approval step listed every earlier task with 'Approve' and 'Approve with comments'; after approving, the run showed 'Workflow run completed'. Reports showed the open run under Active Workflow Runs (2 of 7 tasks, on track) and the finished one under Recently Completed. The sample 'Start Here' workflow also counts as a run.

What they praise
  • • It is the tool people reach for to turn SOPs into workflows that actually get followed: 4.6 on G2 across 460 reviews, over 90% would recommend.
  • • Onboarding, compliance and operations teams use it daily, which is a stronger signal than a high score alone.
  • • TrustRadius reviewers describe the same use case over and over, and it is narrower than 'project management': anything the business does repeatedly. Recurring work, checklists with accountability, and a reporting dashboard that shows where a process stalls.
What they complain about
  • • There is no public per-seat price. You get a 14-day trial and a sales quote, and a 2025 change made every active user billable on new accounts, so headcount growth changes the bill in ways you cannot model up front.
  • • The price figures circulating on aggregator sites appear to be package totals rather than per-user rates, and are unconfirmed. Do not budget from them.
  • • For a team that only wants simple checklists, some reviewers find the depth gets in the way.
  • • The one gripe that repeats on TrustRadius is native integrations: reviewers who otherwise rate it top marks want more apps connected without going through an automation layer.

Where the ratings disagree. Read TrustRadius with the sample in mind: its Process Street reviews cluster in November 2023, nearly all carry an 'Incentivized' label, and one of them is written by an account executive at Process Street itself. When the paid plan is worth it

You cannot weigh this on price: every tier reads Contact sales. What it publishes are the ceilings, and those are the upgrade. Startup gives you 5 users, 10 guests, 5,000 Data Set records, 10 automation apps, 100 automation actions a month, 50 API calls a month and a 5 MB cap per file. Pro raises the file cap to 100 MB, doubles Data Set records to 10,000, opens all automation apps, and turns users, actions and API calls into custom numbers. Enterprise repeats that with unlimited API calls. Two of those bite first. Fifty API calls a month is a test allowance, not an integration, and 100 automation actions is about three a day: the first workflow you automate spends it. Startup is also gated by who you are: "businesses that have less than 15 employees and under $2 million in revenue."

What upgrading does not fix is the missing number: Pro's limits are custom, so moving up trades published ceilings for negotiated ones and you still leave without a figure. Use the 14-day Pro trial, no card needed, to measure your real action and API volume: the only thing you can carry into the sales call.

How to actually use Process Street

The shortest route to real value is the AI Document Importer. Process Street's help docs describe clicking + New in the top right corner, choosing Workflow, then Import Document, dropping in an existing SOP and clicking Generate Workflow. The importer accepts PDF, DOCX, HTML, CSV and image files, with a file size limit of 25 MB and an image limit of 20 MB, and returns tasks with form fields, approvals, stop tasks and role assignments rather than a flat checklist.

Every new account starts on a complimentary 14-day trial of the Pro plan with no credit card, so the sensible move is to import a process people run and push a live run through it inside that window. The vendor's own starter lesson budgets 10 minutes to create a workflow. The usual stall is seats and permissions. Invitations sit under the profile menu at Settings, Users & Guests. The Startup tier includes 5 users and 10 guests, and the docs state that once a limit is reached each extra person is billed pro-rata at the moment of invitation.

Frequently asked questions

Does Process Street's trial require a credit card?

Sign-up check 2026-10-23: card not requested in this account; trial length confirmed: 14 days.

What does Process Street cost?

They do not say. Every tier reads Contact sales, and two monthly captures plus our own reads on 17 August and 30 September 2026 found no amount on the page. The numbers circulating elsewhere are third-party estimates, so treat a quote as the only real figure and ask for it per member and per month.

Who qualifies for the Startup plan?

Process Street describes it as a simplified Pro plan and states the eligibility on the page: businesses with fewer than 15 employees and under $2 million in revenue. The hard limits that come with it are 5 users and 10 guests, 5,000 Data Set records, 10 automation apps, 100 automation actions a month and 50 public API calls a month.

Can I try it before talking to sales?

Yes, on Pro: a 14-day free trial with no credit card required, per their pricing page on 17 August 2026. That is the practical route to a number, since the trial lets you size the users and automation volume you actually need before the sales conversation prices them.

Who counts as a paid seat?

More roles than the plan cards suggest. Process Street's help article What is a User? (updated 28 April 2026, read 6 October 2026) says that in organisations created on or after 1 August 2025, Users are billable alongside Admins and Builders, and all three count toward your seat total; in older organisations only Admins and Builders are billed. The Guest article (updated 4 September 2026) notes that guests are counted as a seat in your subscription and that promoting one partway through the billing cycle is charged pro rata. The Startup card, by contrast, lists 10 guests next to its 5 users, so whether an external guest is a paid seat is something to settle in the quote: not confirmed. Plan changes go through support, and only an Administrator can request one.

What happens to my workflows if I cancel, and what can I export?

Cancelling keeps your data. The help article on deleting an account or cancelling (updated 15 September 2026, read 6 October 2026) says you cancel from the billing page under Manage subscription, and your workflows and workflow runs remain in the account so you can subscribe again later; deleting the organisation is a separate step, scheduled 14 days out and reversible within that window. A trial is cancelled through support. For export, the documentation describes workflows and workflow runs as PDF and the data from all runs of a workflow as a CSV file. An export route for Pages or Data Sets is not described there: not confirmed. Neither article states a refund policy.
