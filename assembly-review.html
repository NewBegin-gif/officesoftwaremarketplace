Assembly Review (2026)

By Daan Haket · Tested hands-on · written with an AI assistant · who does what

Active contacts bite first: Starter stops at 50, and the step to 500 costs $70 more a month on yearly billing ($90 monthly).

Best for

agencies and professional services firms who want one branded place for clients to message, sign, pay and share files instead of four tools and an email thread.

Skip it if

you have a handful of clients and already use a shared drive and an invoicing tool, because the value here is the single branded portal and that only matters once clients get confused about where things live.

Assembly, the client-portal platform at assembly.com, publishes five tiers. Free, a free-forever plan: $0 with 5 active contacts, 1 internal user, 50 build credits a month and 3 apps. Starter: $29 a month billed yearly or $49 monthly, 50 active contacts, 1 internal user. Professional: $99 billed yearly or $139 monthly, 500 active contacts, 3 internal users included and each extra one +$29 a month yearly or +$39 monthly.

Advanced: $499 billed yearly or $599 monthly, unlimited active contacts, 5 internal users included and each extra one +$59 or +$79. Enterprise is quoted after a demo. Every paid plan includes 200 build credits a month and 5 apps; extra credits are $0.60 each up to 10,000 a month, unused credits roll over for one month, and extra apps are $5 a month each.

Full pricing notes (2 more paragraphs)

If you bill clients through Assembly, cards cost 2.9% + $0.30 on every plan and ACH 1% (capped at $10 from Professional up); the table also lists store fees of 2.0%, 1.5%, 1% and 0.7% from Free to Advanced, invoices at +0.7% down to +0.3% and recurring subscriptions at +1.1% down to +0.5%. The FAQ says there are no setup, hosting or per-client fees, no contracts on self-serve plans, and downgrades take effect at the end of the billing period.

Correction: until 28 September 2026 this field described a different company's employee-recognition plan at $3 per user; that was never the price of Assembly's client portal.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Internal users are the other, and they get dearer as you climb: an extra colleague is $29 a month on Professional and $59 on Advanced (yearly-billed; $39 and $79 monthly), so the more expensive plan makes each additional person more expensive too. A newer third meter is build credits for the AI app builder — 200 a month on every paid plan, $0.60 each beyond that.

When the paid plan is worth it

Starter is a real plan rather than a demo: $29 a month billed yearly ($49 monthly) carries the CRM, the branded client experience, messaging, billing, contracts and e-signature, file sharing, forms and tasks, plus the API, Zapier, Make and the MCP connector. What it does not carry is room — 1 internal user and 50 active contacts — so what pushes you up is usually a count. The Free plan below it (5 contacts, 3 apps) is for trying the portal, not for running a firm on it.

Professional at $99 billed yearly ($139 monthly) is where the portal starts looking like yours: a custom domain, a custom email domain, the Assembly badge removed, app-visibility settings and the automation builder, with 3 internal users and 500 contacts. Advanced at $499 ($599) is a compliance tier more than a capacity one: audit log, client access permissions, enforced MFA and HIPAA with a BAA, plus unlimited contacts and 5 users.

Three things paying more does not fix. Extra internal users cost more on the pricier plan — +$29 a month on Professional, +$59 on Advanced, yearly-billed — the reverse of how a tier ladder usually works. Build credits stay at 200 a month on every paid tier, so heavy app building costs $0.60 a credit whichever plan you are on. And the card fee is 2.9% + $0.30 on every plan; only the smaller store, invoice and subscription percentages fall as you climb. Read on 28 September 2026.

How to actually use Assembly

Assembly's own documentation puts two setup steps in an order that matters, and your plan decides whether you can follow it. A custom domain is a Professional feature ($99 a month billed yearly, $139 monthly), and the domain guide advises setting one up before you invite clients — otherwise, in its words, they will need to log in again. A firm that starts on Starter and moves the portal to portal.yourfirm.com later pays for that ordering with a second round of client logins.

Connecting it is Settings > Domains, a CNAME added at your registrar, then 5 minutes to 24 hours of waiting; the docs single out Cloudflare, where the proxy — the orange cloud — has to be off. Getting clients in runs three ways: one at a time, a CSV bulk import, or direct sign-up. The import is where the documented limits bite — First name, Last name and Email are required (or a Full name field that has to contain both), 200 rows per CSV file, and a comma anywhere in a field value makes the import fail.

The last step asks for Import only or Import and invite, and clients left in Not invited status get no email notifications at all, which the docs suggest using on purpose while you upload files and assign forms first. Automations need Professional: the September 2026 comparison table leaves the automation builder off Free and Starter. The automations guide we read on 7 August 2026 listed three templates — assign a contract or a form when a client first logs in, and upload files or folders when a client or company is created — plus a build-from-scratch option.

The AI app builder, documented as a private beta then, is on every plan by 28 September 2026 and metered in build credits: planning an app is free, failed builds cost nothing, and the pricing FAQ puts a typical app at a few dozen credits. Every limit and timing above is what the vendor documents, not something we measured.

What does Assembly cost?

There is a free plan with 5 active contacts, 1 internal user and 3 apps. The paid plans, read on 28 September 2026: Starter $29 a month billed yearly or $49 monthly, Professional $99 or $139, Advanced $499 or $599, and Enterprise quoted after a demo. Extra internal users are +$29 a month on Professional and +$59 on Advanced when billed yearly ($39 and $79 monthly). Self-serve plans have no contract, and downgrades take effect at the end of the billing period.

What does Assembly take when my clients pay through it?

Card payments are 2.9% + $0.30 on every plan, and ACH is 1%, capped at $10 per payment from Professional up. The comparison table read on 28 September 2026 also lists store fees of 2.0% on Free, 1.5% on Starter, 1% on Professional and 0.7% on Advanced, with invoices at +0.7% down to +0.3% and recurring subscriptions at +1.1% down to +0.5%. The FAQ says there are no setup, hosting or per-client fees. Run those percentages against your monthly billing before you pick a tier.

Do I need to budget for the AI app builder?

Only if you build a lot. Every paid plan includes 200 build credits a month (Free gets 50), unused credits roll over for one extra month, and planning an app or a failed build costs nothing; the FAQ says a typical app takes a few dozen credits. Beyond that, credits are $0.60 each, up to 10,000 a month, at the same rate on every paid plan. Using live apps consumes no credits, and paid plans include 5 apps before the $5-a-month charge per extra app.
