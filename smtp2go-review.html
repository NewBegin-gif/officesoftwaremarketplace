The 50,000 tier beats Professional per email

Seven paid steps, and the price per email does not fall in a straight line down them (re-read on their pricing page on 26 September 2026, with the volume slider walked through every stop). Free is 1,000 emails a month capped at 200 a day, five days of reporting, and no overage permitted: hit the cap and sending stops. Starter is $15 a month for 10,000 or $30 for 50,000.

Professional is $75 for 100,000, $170 for 250,000, $280 for 500,000 and $520 for a million; Premier is quote-only above three million. Annual billing is exactly ten months' money at every step: $150 rather than $180 on the smallest paid plan. Per thousand emails at each plan's own ceiling that reads $1.50, $0.60, $0.75, $0.68, $0.56 and $0.52. The step you would expect to be a discount is the one that is not: moving from Starter's 50,000 to Professional's 100,000 raises the rate per email by a quarter.

What the upgrade buys is the dedicated IP address and the email-testing tools, not cheaper email. The overage line decides the rest. Each tier prices extra thousands at its own rate: $1.20, $1.00, $0.85, $0.70, $0.60, $0.50, so on the 10,000 plan an extra thousand at $1.20 costs less than the thousands you already bought at $1.50. Run the same sum upward and the $30 plan plus overage stays cheaper than Professional until about 95,000 emails a month.

What none of the tiers buys is reputation. Without SPF, DKIM and DMARC configured properly and honest list hygiene, no relay rescues your delivery, and the plan you pick will not be the reason your mail lands.

    How to actually use SMTP2GO

    Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC before you send a single message through it. A relay delivers what your domain has earned, and the most common reason a switch of provider disappoints is that the authentication records came across half-configured. This is the step that decides whether anything else matters. Then use the free tier to test shape rather than volume. A thousand emails a month sounds like room to experiment, but the real constraint is 200 a day and the free plan permits no overage at all: enough to verify that your application connects, your templates render and your bounces are reported, and nothing like enough to test a campaign send.

    Size the plan from the overage rates rather than the headline. Every tier prices extra thousands at its own rate, which makes the arithmetic short: the 10,000 plan plus overage costs the same as the 50,000 plan at about 22,500 emails a month, and the 50,000 plan plus overage stays cheaper than Professional until about 95,000. Move to Professional earlier than that only if you need the dedicated IP address, and if you do, warm it up properly.

    What happens if I send more emails than my plan includes?

    On a paid plan you keep sending and pay per extra thousand at your tier's rate: $1.20 on the 10,000 plan, $1 on 50,000, then $0.85, $0.70, $0.60 and $0.50 on the Professional steps from 100,000 to 1,000,000 (read 26 September 2026). On the free plan extra emails are not permitted, and it is also limited to 200 emails a day, so a send past either limit simply does not go out.

    Do I need Professional for a dedicated IP address?

    Yes. On the pricing page (read 26 September 2026) the dedicated IP address and the email-testing tools appear only on the Professional cards, from $75 a month for 100,000 emails; Starter lists 30 days of reporting, ticket, chat and phone support, subaccounts, SMS messaging and scheduled reporting, but no dedicated IP. Automatic DKIM and SPF, feedback loops and blacklist monitoring are listed for all plans, so authentication itself does not require an upgrade.

    Can I get my money back if SMTP2GO doesn't suit me?

    The pricing page (read 26 September 2026) promises a full refund if you don't love it within 30 days. Invoices are always in US dollars - the EUR, GBP, AUD and NZD figures on the page are approximations - and yearly billing costs ten months' money, $150 instead of $180 on the smallest paid plan. The free plan runs as long as you like, but its toll-free phone and live chat support stop after 14 days.

    Is there a ceiling on overage?

    Yes. SMTP2GO's help article 'Subscription Plan Differences Explained' (updated 8 September 2026, read 17 November 2026) says Starter, Professional and Premier plans allow email overage up to three times your subscription limit, billed in blocks of 1,000. The article does not say what happens to mail beyond that cap, so treat it as not confirmed and move up a plan before a send that large. The free plan permits no overage at all.

    What can be charged on top of the plan?

    Per the 'Billing, Pricing and Plans FAQ' (updated 29 September 2026, read 17 November 2026): email archiving at USD 0.50 per GB per month, extra dedicated IPs at USD 19 per IP per month (Professional and Premier include one), SMS per unit sent plus a number where needed, and longer activity storage beyond the default 30 days, priced in the account. Upgrades take effect immediately with credit for the unused part of the old plan; downgrades wait until the end of the payment cycle. Non-profits get 20% off the subscription and overage after sending proof of status.

    Who on my team can see billing?

    Only Owners. The help article 'Managing Your Team' (updated 3 November 2026, read 17 November 2026) lists three roles: Owner with full access, Admin with full access except subscription billing and plan management, and Reports with reporting only. The free plan allows two team members; paid plans have no limit.
