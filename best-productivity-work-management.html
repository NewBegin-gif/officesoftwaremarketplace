1. ClickUp — One workspace for projects, docs and goals

The 'everything app' for project and work management. Centralize your tasks, docs, sprints, and team communication in one hub.

My take: The everything-app: powerful once configured, overwhelming if you skip the setup week. Read my hands-on review →

Visit ClickUp →

2. SmartSuite — Replacing a stack of scattered spreadsheets

One flexible work management platform to plan, track and automate any process. Projects, CRM and operations side by side — replace a stack of scattered tools with a single source of truth.

My take: The most flexible no-code database of this list — process people love it. Read our full SmartSuite review →

Visit SmartSuite →

3. Reclaim.ai — Calendars that defend your focus time

Auto-schedules tasks, habits and meetings around your stated priorities. Google Calendar-first, so Outlook-heavy teams are out, and the free tier is limited. Paid seats run about $8–10/month on Starter and Plus, roughly 29% cheaper on annual billing.

My take: Set your priorities once and it quietly reshuffles your week — my kind of automation. Read my hands-on review →

Visit Reclaim.ai →

4. SaneBox — Inboxes that filter themselves

Email triage that moves low-priority mail out of the inbox and works with any provider. From $4.54/month for one account on a two-year commitment — $9.49 month to month — up to $22.04 for four accounts with every feature. The honest catch: you are paying monthly to filter mail that modern email clients increasingly sort for free.

My take: AI email triage that needs zero new habits; you only see what matters. Read my hands-on review →

Visit SaneBox →

5. Process Street — Recurring work as trackable checklists

Recurring work as trackable checklists and SOPs, with approvals and conditional logic. Pricing is opaque: Startup begins near $100/month for up to five members and steps up to a custom Pro tier in the $1,500/month range, with seat minimums on top.

My take: I reviewed it hands-on: turning SOPs into living checklists beats static docs every time. Read my hands-on review →

Visit Process Street →

6. Trainual — Onboarding & making yourself replaceable

Documents SOPs and runs new-hire onboarding, with AI that drafts and updates training docs from your own account context. Not publicly priced: all four plans are quote-gated behind a demo, and the only figure the pricing page publishes is a one-time implementation fee of $1,000.

My take: The playbook tool — document how your business runs so it runs without you. Read my hands-on review →

Visit Trainual →

7. WebCatalog — Taming 40 browser tabs into apps

Turn web apps into tidy desktop apps and manage multiple accounts side by side. Less tab chaos, more focus — your whole stack organized in one place.

My take: Small tool, daily payoff: web apps become tidy desktop apps with separate accounts. Read our full WebCatalog review →

Visit WebCatalog →
