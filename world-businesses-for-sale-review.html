Pricing (2026)

Broker plans: Basic £395 (3% commission, 6-month contract), Standard £749 (2% commission, 9 months), Premium £1,500 (1% commission, 12-month contract, stays live until sold with free renewals) — every tier states commission is due only if they introduce the buyer. Or no-commission pay-as-you-go listings (inc. VAT): Basic £49, Standard £75 or Premium £99 for 30-day access.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Only Premium stays live until sold with free renewals, so on the lower tiers a business that has not sold needs a paid renewal, and for a slow sale £1,500 once can beat £749 twice. Every tier says commission is payable only when they introduce the buyer, which is exactly the clause to read in the contract before you sign. If you want no commission at all, the pay-as-you-go plans start at £49 for 30 days — far cheaper up front, but those are listings, not broker introductions. As with any marketplace, the fee buys exposure, not a guaranteed sale.

Which broker tier wins depends on your sale price

Browsing is free for buyers; sellers choose between two quite different tracks (2026, re-verified 2026-08-03). The broker plans carry a listing fee and a commission: Basic £395 with 3% on a six-month contract, Standard £749 with 2% over nine months, and Premium £1,500 with 1% over twelve months, where Premium stays live until sold with free renewals. Every tier states that commission is payable only if they introduce the buyer.

Because the fee falls as the commission falls, which tier is cheapest depends entirely on what your business sells for, and the crossovers are worth having in front of you. Below about £35,400, Basic is cheapest. Between £35,400 and £75,100, Standard wins. Above £75,100, Premium is cheapest — and the gap widens fast: on a £500,000 sale, Basic costs £15,395 all in, Standard £10,749 and Premium £6,500. If you are selling anything of size, the most expensive listing fee is the cheapest deal.

The contract lengths reinforce that. Only Premium stays live until sold with free renewals, so on the lower tiers an unsold business needs a paid renewal — and £749 twice is £1,498, which is a pound under Premium's £1,500 for half the commission rate. For anything but a quick sale, Premium is doing better than it looks. Now the clause that decides thousands of pounds, and the reason to read the actual contract rather than the pricing page.

Commission is payable only when they introduce the buyer — so if you find your own buyer, you pay the listing fee and nothing else. On a £500,000 sale that single word is worth between £5,000 and £15,000, and everything depends on how 'introduce' is defined. Does a buyer who found your listing on their site count as their introduction? Almost certainly, but get it defined in writing before you sign anything, because the page will not define it for you.

The other track avoids all of this. Pay-as-you-go listings carry no commission at all: Basic £49, Standard £75 or Premium £99 for 30 days including VAT. There is 10% off on a flexible monthly subscription and 20% off on a six-month plan — £235.20, £360.00 or £475.20 — and you keep 100% of the sale price. On that £500,000 example, six months of PAYG Premium is £475 against Premium broker's £6,500.

The difference is what you are buying: those are listings, not broker introductions, and the fee buys exposure rather than a sale. Free 24/7 chat guidance is available before you commit either way.

How to actually use World Businesses for Sale

Estimate your sale price first, because it decides the tier. Below about £35,400, broker Basic is cheapest; between £35,400 and £75,100, Standard; above £75,100, Premium — and on a £500,000 sale Premium costs £6,500 all in against Basic's £15,395. The highest listing fee is the cheapest outcome for anything of size. Get one clause defined in writing before you sign a broker plan: what counts as them introducing the buyer.

Commission is payable only on their introduction, so if you find your own buyer you owe the listing fee alone — and on a mid-sized sale that definition is worth five figures. Decide honestly whether you want a broker at all. The pay-as-you-go track carries no commission — from £49 for 30 days, or £235.20 to £475.20 for six months with the 20% discount — and you keep the whole sale price.

What you give up is the introduction, not the exposure. If you already know your likely buyers, that trade is an easy one. Use the free chat guidance before committing either way, and note that only broker Premium stays live until sold, so on the lower tiers budget for a paid renewal if the sale is slow.
