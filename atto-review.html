Atto Review (2026)

By Daan Haket · Tested hands-on · written with an AI assistant · who does what

Simple and honest pricing — the real conversation with any field-team tracker is the human one: GPS tracking of employees needs transparency and often consent (rules differ by country), and adoption dies the day the crew feels surveilled rather than served.

Best for

hourly and mobile crews (construction, cleaning, field service) that want dead-simple clock-in/out, GPS-verified timesheets and payroll-ready exports.

Skip it if

office/knowledge teams (project-based trackers like Toggl fit better), or anywhere the team hasn't been brought along on why.

We tested it · 2026-10-23

Tested by our team on 23 October 2026 during the free trial, without a card, without any payroll, Google or Microsoft connection, without installing the app and without inviting team members: Daan created the account and our AI assistant ran the test in Daan's browser. We clocked two work sessions of about two minutes from the browser with a gap of about a minute between them, timing every click with the browser clock, and compared the timesheet with what we measured.

How we tested

  1. Read the trial indicator and Team Activity dashboard
  2. Used the browser Time Clock to clock in
  3. Timed a first clock-in and clock-out session
  4. Left an unclocked interval before timing a second session
  5. Compared Reports > Timesheets with our stopwatch log
  6. Inspected the time-card editing and download controls
  • Clocking in from the browser needed no app: the Time Clock page has a large CLOCK IN button, after which the status read 'On the Clock', location 'Web Clock In', job code Uncategorized, with options to add notes, take a break or add a job.
  • We measured 132.7 s and 131.5 s of work (264.2 s, 4.40 minutes) with 71.4 s not clocked in between. Reports > Timesheets for that week showed Total Paid Hours 4m and Regular 4m, with no break counted: the gap rightly stayed out of the worked time. The overview shows whole minutes, so seconds cannot be checked there.
  • On the day of sign-up the sidebar already said '13 days left in trial', against '14 day free trial' on the website. No card was asked.

What Atto does

Time tracking and timesheets for hourly and mobile teams. Track hours, breaks and locations with GPS and turn timesheets into payroll-ready reports.

When the paid plan is worth it

Atto has no free tier: after the trial you pick from a paid ladder, and the gate that matters is GPS. Starter, $6.95 per user/month billed monthly or $5.65 billed annually, covers clock-in/out, job codes and basic overtime, and records where people clock in and out, but not real-time location tracking or a location history. That starts on Plus, $9.95 monthly or $7.95 annually. If you came to Atto for GPS-verified timesheets, which is the reason most field crews look at it, Starter is not the product you had in mind and Plus is the honest entry price.

Advanced at $12.95 monthly, $10.75 annually, adds the tablet Kiosk, mileage tracking and job-specific pay rates. Expert at $19.95 monthly, $16.55 annually, is mostly service — priority support, personalized onboarding — plus API access, so it only pays back if you actually need to push data somewhere Atto does not already export to. Upgrading fixes none of the things that break rollouts: every phone still needs continuous location permission, and the crew still has to accept being located. Annual billing saves about 20% on Plus but only 17% on Advanced and Expert.

How to actually use Atto

Sign up at dashboard.attotime.com, fill in name, company, industry and password, and the 14-day free trial starts at signup with no card. The documented order is: invite the team from Settings > Invite New Members, by email or by sharing the account link. Then set company settings for location tracking, workweek and overtime rules, breaks and mileage, and assign Admin, Manager or Employee roles and pay rates under Settings > Manage Team.

Atto says you can invite first and tune the rest later, so a crew can be clocking in the same day.

Common Atto setup mistakes

Where Atto setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above.

The friction is on the phone, not the dashboard.

  • Atto's clock-in troubleshooting lists the permissions each phone needs: on iOS, Always or While Using the App with precise location on; on Android, Allow all the time or while-in-use.
  • Which one depends on whether your account runs continuous or clock-in-only tracking.
  • Miss it and managers just see Location Permissions OFF in Team Activity.
  • Other quiet blockers: an account can require a Job Code before clock-in, and job-site boundaries will refuse a clock-in from outside the perimeter.

Frequently asked questions

Does Atto's cheapest plan show where my crew is during the shift?

No, only where they clocked in and out. Read 24 September 2026 from the plan data behind attotime.com/pricing, Starter ($5.65 per user a month billed annually, $6.95 monthly) includes clock-in and clock-out locations and job-site clock-in restrictions, which stop someone clocking in from outside a site's boundary. Real-time location tracking and the location history report start on Plus, at $7.95 annually or $9.95 monthly. Automatic mileage tracking and the tablet time clock kiosk start one tier higher, on Advanced ($10.75 or $12.95). So if the question is whether people arrived on site, Starter answers it; if it is where they went during the day, you need Plus.

What does Atto's payroll add-on cost on top of the plan?

$5.95 per payroll user a month plus a $39.95 monthly base fee, read 24 September 2026 on Atto's pricing page, with unlimited payroll runs - and only for US-based businesses. The base fee is what makes it expensive for small crews. For 10 people the add-on is $59.50 plus $39.95, or $99.45 a month, on top of the time-tracking plan; on Plus billed annually that plan is another $79.50, so about $179 a month in total. Outside the US the add-on is not available at all.

Will Atto charge me when the 14-day trial ends?

Not unless you choose to subscribe. Read 24 September 2026, the pricing FAQ says the trial needs no credit card, and after 14 days you are asked to buy a subscription to keep using Atto rather than being charged automatically. The same FAQ says there are no hidden charges or usage limits: you pay per user in the account. That makes the choice mainly monthly against annual. Annual billing saves about 19% on Starter, 20% on Plus and 17% on Advanced and Expert, and Expert ($16.55 annually, $19.95 monthly) is booked through a sales call rather than a checkout.
