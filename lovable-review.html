We tested it · 2026-09-26

On 26 September 2026 we built the same class-booking web app in Lovable, Replit and Emergent from one identical prompt, each on its free plan, and checked every result against 18 tests we wrote down before the first build. We changed no code: the builder only got our prompt. Lovable paid us nothing when we ran the test; it accepted us into its affiliate programme on 28 September 2026, and these findings were recorded before that.

  • Lovable built and published the app in about ten minutes and used 4 of the 10 credits on a new account. It asked twice, to create the project and to publish, and published to a lovable.app address that stays online on the free plan with an 'Edit with Lovable' badge.
  • It was the only one of the three whose first version held the limit when ten people booked an eight-spot class at the same moment: eight accepted, two refused with 'Sorry, this class is full.' The other two accepted all ten.
  • It checked input on the server as well as in the form (a name of only spaces and a bad email were refused), kept the admin password out of the page and scripts, and prefixed CSV values starting with = + - or @ so a spreadsheet does not run them as formulas.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Lovable prices each build message by the work it turns out to involve, from about half a credit for a styling tweak to two credits for a page with images, so a credit budget is an estimate, not a price list. The second catch is easy to miss: the app you publish runs its database, hosting and AI features on a separate monthly Cloud grant of 20 credits. Lovable's own documentation says a published app stays live when those credits run out, but its backend services and AI features pause, so an app that works on day one can stop saving data later in the month.

The larger Pro tiers are no cheaper per credit until 1,200 credits a month, so buying ahead saves nothing below that.

When the paid plan is worth it

Pay when one of three limits bites. The free plan's 30 build credits a month are enough to build and publish a small app (our booking app took 4), but not to keep changing it. A custom domain and removing the Lovable badge both need Pro. And if the app's backend runs out of the 20 free Cloud credits a month, its data and AI features pause until you top up.

Pro at 25 euros for 100 credits is the step for all three; buy the tier you actually use, because every tier up to 800 credits costs the same 25 cents a credit and the first discount is 2% at 1,200. Business costs double for the same 100 credits and is only worth it for SSO, internal-only publishing or a security center.

How to actually use Lovable

Write what you want as a numbered list of requirements rather than a paragraph, and say what to leave out; in our test Lovable followed eight numbered requirements and skipped payments, email and accounts as asked. Starting from the dashboard, it asks to create a project and later asks before publishing; both are one click. Expect it to fill gaps itself: it invented a studio name, a temporary admin password and a time zone, and said so in its summary, so replace those before anyone else sees the app.

Lovable itself advises running its security scan before sharing a link widely. Then test the awkward case yourself: two people taking the last spot at the same moment, or whatever the equivalent is in your app.

Frequently asked questions

How many credits does Lovable's free plan actually give me?

Thirty build credits a month, front-loaded. Lovable's credits documentation (read 27 September 2026) says Free gets 5 build credits a day, capped at 30 per calendar month, so the grant arrives over the first six days and then stops until the 1st of the next month at 00:00 UTC. Separate monthly grants of 20 credits for hosting and backend (Cloud) and 4 for AI features cover the published app. Pro and Business also get the 5 daily build credits, with no monthly cap, on top of their monthly allowance.

Do unused Lovable credits roll over?

Partly. The documentation (read 27 September 2026) says unused monthly plan credits roll over as long as the subscription stays active, but keep their original expiry: two months after issue on monthly billing, one month after the annual period ends on yearly billing. Top-up credits, sold on Pro and Business only, last 12 months. The daily build credits and the Cloud and AI grants never roll over.

What happens to my Lovable app if I run out of credits?

The site stays up; the backend may not. Per the documentation (read 27 September 2026) the published pages keep serving, but AI features stop and an app relying on Lovable Cloud for its database, storage or login can pause. That only happens when the workspace has no credits left at all: once the Cloud grant is used, hosting draws on your general credits. On Free there are few of those, which is why an app can stall late in the month. Everything resumes when credits return, by top-up or the next grant.

Can I reconnect the same GitHub repository after disconnecting it?

No. Lovable's GitHub integration guide (docs.lovable.dev, read 21 October 2026) lists reconnecting to the same repository after disconnecting as unsupported: Lovable creates a new repository when you reconnect. Treat the disconnect as one-way and keep your own clone of the repository before you do it.

Does Lovable keep every GitHub branch in sync?

No, one at a time. The same guide (read 21 October 2026) says Lovable only syncs one branch at a time, so work on other branches does not flow back into the editor until you switch the synced branch. Teams that rely on several long-running branches should plan for that.

Does chatting with Lovable use up my build credits?

Not first. Lovable's credits-and-usage documentation (read 21 October 2026) says messages in Chat mode follow their own pricing, with a per-message price and a daily chat allowance included on Free, Pro and Business; only after that allowance is used up does chat draw on the daily build credits. The page does not state how large the allowance is, and Lovable calls the chat pricing and the monthly Cloud, AI and connector grants temporary and subject to change.
